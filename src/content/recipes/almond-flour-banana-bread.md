There is a special kind of disappointment that comes with gluten-free baking — a loaf that looks promising, slices into something dry and crumbly, and tastes like a compromise. This almond flour banana bread is the opposite of that. It is tender, moist, and nutty in the best way, with a golden, slightly crackly top and a crumb so soft you would never guess there is no wheat flour in it.

Almond flour does something wonderful in banana bread: its natural oils keep the loaf moist for days, and its mild, buttery flavour pairs perfectly with ripe bananas. Add a touch of maple syrup, a whisper of cinnamon, and you have a loaf that works for a weekday breakfast, an afternoon snack, or a dessert with a scoop of vanilla ice cream.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

This is a genuinely gluten-free banana bread that nobody at the table will call "the gluten-free one." The almond flour gives a rich, tender crumb that stays moist for days — no dry edges, no crumbly slices. It comes together in one bowl plus one for the dry ingredients, uses just three bananas, and bakes up beautifully every time. It is also naturally dairy-free if you use coconut oil, and the maple syrup keeps the sweetness gentle and warm rather than sugary.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** As with any banana bread, the riper the better. Heavily speckled or black skins mean the bananas are sweet enough that a quarter cup of maple syrup is all the added sweetness the loaf needs. Mash them until mostly smooth — a few small lumps are fine.

**Blanched almond flour.** This is the star, and the type matters. Blanched almond flour is finely ground from almonds with the skins removed, and it gives a soft, cake-like crumb. Almond meal (skins on, coarser grind) will work, but the loaf will be denser and darker. Measure by spooning the flour into your measuring cup and levelling it off — almond flour packs down easily, and too much of it makes a heavy loaf.

**Tapioca starch.** Just a quarter cup, but it does important work: it binds the crumb and gives the loaf a little spring that almond flour alone lacks. If you don't have tapioca starch, arrowroot starch works the same way.

**Eggs.** Three eggs do the structural work that gluten would normally do, binding the crumb and giving the loaf its lift. Room temperature eggs blend into the batter more evenly — set them in a bowl of warm water for 5 minutes if you forgot to take them out.

**Maple syrup.** The only sweetener besides the bananas. It adds a warm, caramel note that honey or white sugar doesn't quite match. Honey works as a 1:1 swap if that's what you have.

**Coconut oil or butter.** Either works. Coconut oil keeps the loaf dairy-free and adds the faintest tropical note; butter gives a richer, more classic flavour. Whichever you choose, let it cool slightly before mixing so it doesn't cook the eggs.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

Almond flour batter behaves a little differently from wheat batter, so a few technique notes make all the difference. First, whisk the dry ingredients well — almond flour clumps, and any clump that survives into the oven becomes a dry pocket. Rub the lumps out with your fingers as you whisk.

Second, don't overmix. Once the dry ingredients meet the wet, stir just until no dry streaks remain. Almond flour doesn't develop gluten, so you won't get a tough loaf from overmixing the way wheat batter does — but over-stirring still knocks out air and makes the crumb dense.

Third, trust the bake time. The top will look done at 45 minutes, but the centre of an almond flour loaf needs the full 50–55 minutes to set. The toothpick test is your friend: you want a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. If the top is browning faster than the centre is baking, tent loosely with foil and give it the last 15 minutes.

The hardest step is the last one: waiting. Almond flour bread is fragile while warm and slices much more cleanly once completely cool. Give it the full cooling time on the rack — the crumb firms up as it cools, and your patience is rewarded with neat, intact slices.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. Almond flour loaves are delicate, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out without it breaking.
- **Weigh the almond flour if you can.** 2 1/2 cups should be about 250 g. If you only have cups, spoon the flour in and level — never scoop straight from the bag, which packs it down and makes the loaf heavy.
- **Check your bananas.** If your bananas are only lightly speckled, the loaf will be less sweet and a little less moist. Bake unripe bananas unpeeled at 300°F for 15–20 minutes until the skins blacken, then let them cool before mashing.
- **For a taller loaf,** make sure your baking soda is fresh — it should fizz vigorously in a drop of vinegar. Old baking soda is the most common cause of a flat almond flour loaf.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Chocolate chip.** Fold in 1/2 cup of dark chocolate chips — they melt into little pockets of chocolate against the nutty crumb. Dairy-free chips keep the loaf dairy-free.

**Walnut crunch.** Fold in 1/2 cup of chopped toasted walnuts and sprinkle a few over the top before baking. The crunch against the tender crumb is wonderful.

**Blueberry almond.** Fold in 3/4 cup of fresh or frozen blueberries (toss frozen ones in a teaspoon of tapioca starch first so they don't sink). The berries add juicy bursts of tartness.

**Spiced chai.** Add 1/2 teaspoon of ground cardamom, 1/4 teaspoon of ground ginger, and a pinch of black pepper alongside the cinnamon for a warming chai-spiced version.

**Lemon almond.** Add the zest of one lemon to the wet ingredients and swap the vanilla for almond extract (use 1/2 teaspoon — it's potent). Bright, fragrant, and lovely with tea.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm them gently — 10 seconds in the microwave or a few minutes in a low oven. The loaf is wonderful plain, but a smear of almond butter, a drizzle of honey, or a spoonful of Greek yogurt turns it into a proper breakfast. For dessert, try it warm with vanilla ice cream or a dollop of whipped cream and fresh berries. With coffee or chai, it is hard to beat.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Because almond flour is rich in natural oils, this loaf stays moist longer than wheat banana bread. Keep it wrapped tightly at room temperature for up to 2 days, then move it to the fridge for up to 5 days — it slices beautifully cold, straight from the fridge. To freeze, wrap the cooled loaf whole, or wrap individual slices in plastic wrap and freeze in a bag for up to 3 months. Thaw slices at room temperature for about 30 minutes, or warm them in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes to bring back that just-baked softness.
