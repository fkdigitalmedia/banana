Eggnog and banana bread were made for each other — the eggnog brings cream, eggs and warm nutmeg to a loaf that already wanted all three. This banana bread is soft, custardy and spiced like Christmas morning, with a crackly nutmeg-sugar top that shatters gently under the knife. It tastes like the holidays without trying too hard: no frosting, no fuss, just a rich, fragrant loaf that fills the kitchen with the smell of nutmeg and brown sugar while it bakes.

This is the loaf to bake in late November and December, when the eggnog cartons appear in the shops and the bananas on the counter have gone properly speckled. It is wonderful for a holiday breakfast table, a gift wrapped in parchment and ribbon, or a quiet slice with a mug of coffee on a cold morning.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

The eggnog does real work here — it replaces the milk and adds cream, eggs and spice in one pour, so the crumb comes out richer and more custardy than a standard banana bread without any extra effort. Freshly grated nutmeg is the star spice: warm, sweet and a little mysterious, it perfumes the whole loaf in a way pre-ground nutmeg never quite manages. The nutmeg-sugar topping bakes into a thin, crackly crust that gives every slice a delicate crunch on top and a soft, moist crumb underneath. It is a one-bowl, 15-minute-hands-on bake that tastes like you spent all afternoon on it, and it is endlessly adaptable: white chocolate chips, toasted pecans, dried cranberries — all of them belong here.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black-skinned bananas are essential — they bring the sweetness and moisture this loaf needs. Mash until mostly smooth; a few small lumps add nice texture. Measure the mash: 1 1/4 cups is the target. Too much banana makes a wet batter, and a wet batter bakes up dense instead of custardy.

**Eggnog.** This is the ingredient that makes the loaf. Use full-fat, good-quality store-bought eggnog, or homemade eggnog cooled to room temperature. The eggnog must be at room temperature before you mix it in — cold eggnog will make the melted butter seize into little flecks, and you will see them as pale spots in the finished crumb. If you only have low-fat eggnog, the loaf will still work, but it will be a touch less rich.

**Freshly grated nutmeg.** This is the single most important flavour in the loaf, and it is worth grating it fresh. Pre-ground nutmeg loses its volatile oils quickly and tastes flat and dusty; a whole nutmeg grated on a microplane is warm, sweet and almost floral. Keep whole nutmegs in the cupboard — they last for years and cost pennies per loaf.

**Brown sugar.** Light brown sugar sweetens the crumb and adds a gentle molasses note that leans into the eggnog instead of competing with it. Pack it firmly into the measuring cup — a loose scoop can be a quarter-cup short.

**Rum extract.** The warm, boozy note that makes the loaf taste like eggnog rather than just spiced banana bread — and it is alcohol-free, so the loaf is safe for everyone. If you prefer real dark rum, 1 tablespoon is enough; most of the alcohol bakes off, but use the extract if you are baking for children.

**Cinnamon and cloves.** Just enough to warm the loaf without turning it into a spice cake. The cinnamon leans into the nutmeg, and the cloves add a quiet, festive depth in the background. Measure the cloves carefully — a heavy hand makes the loaf taste medicinal.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

There is almost nothing to this loaf, and that is the point. Start by bringing the eggnog to room temperature — set it out on the counter while the oven heats, or warm it very gently for a few seconds in the microwave. Cold eggnog plus melted butter equals seized butter flecks, and that is the one avoidable mistake in this recipe.

Whisk the wet ingredients smooth in one bowl: mashed bananas, eggnog, melted butter, brown sugar, eggs, rum extract and vanilla. It should look creamy and glossy. In a separate bowl, whisk the dry ingredients together — flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, and the spices. Grate the nutmeg directly into the dry bowl so none of its fragrance escapes.

Fold the dry into the wet until just combined — a few streaks of flour are fine; over-stirring develops gluten and bakes up tough instead of tender. Pour the batter into the lined pan, smooth the top, and sprinkle the nutmeg-sugar topping evenly over the surface. This is what bakes into the crackly, golden crust, so do not skip it and do not clump it in one spot.

Start checking at 55 minutes: a toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The eggnog keeps this loaf extra moist, so trust the toothpick, not the clock — a loaf that looks done at 55 minutes can still be wet in the centre. The top should be deep golden and crackly; if it is browning before the centre is done, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes.

Cooling matters. Let the loaf cool 15 minutes in the pan, then lift it out and cool a full 45 minutes on the rack before slicing, or the custardy crumb will compress and the slices will smear. Dust with a little extra freshly grated nutmeg just before serving — it is the finishing touch that makes the loaf smell like Christmas when you cut into it.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Bring the eggnog to room temperature.** Cold eggnog makes the melted butter seize into little flecks. Set the carton out while the oven heats, or warm it gently for a few seconds in the microwave.
- **Grate the nutmeg fresh.** A whole nutmeg on a microplane is warm, sweet and almost floral; pre-ground nutmeg tastes flat and dusty by comparison. This is the single biggest flavour upgrade you can make to this loaf.
- **Measure the mashed banana.** Three bananas of any size is not a measurement — 1 1/4 cups is. Too much banana makes a wet batter, and a wet batter bakes up dense instead of custardy.
- **Trust the toothpick, not the clock.** The eggnog keeps this loaf extra moist, so a loaf that looks done at 55 minutes can still be wet in the centre. Look for a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.
- **Don't skip the topping.** The nutmeg-sugar sprinkle is what bakes into the crackly, golden crust. Without it the top stays pale and plain — and the crackle is half the pleasure of the first slice.
- **Don't slice warm.** The custardy crumb needs time to set or it compresses under the knife. Forty-five minutes on the rack is the hardest part of this recipe — and the most important.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**White chocolate eggnog.** Fold in 3/4 cup of white chocolate chips with the dry ingredients. They melt into creamy pockets that suit the eggnog perfectly — this is the version to bake for people who love the loaf already.

**Toasted pecan eggnog.** Fold in 1/2 cup of chopped toasted pecans. They add crunch against the soft, custardy crumb and a buttery note that leans into the eggnog. Toast them 7-8 minutes at 350°F first, until fragrant.

**Cranberry eggnog.** Fold in 1/2 cup of dried cranberries. They are tart, chewy and festive — a lovely red fleck through the golden crumb, and perfect for a Christmas table.

**Bourbon eggnog.** Swap the rum extract for 1 tablespoon of bourbon and add 1/2 cup of chopped toasted walnuts. The bourbon deepens the caramel notes and the walnuts add a rugged crunch. Use the extract version if you are baking for children.

**Eggnog banana muffins.** Divide the batter between 12 lined muffin cups, sprinkle each with the nutmeg-sugar topping, and bake at 350°F for 20-23 minutes, until a toothpick comes out with a few moist crumbs. They are perfect for a holiday breakfast or a lunchbox treat.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm a slice for 10 seconds in the microwave so the crumb turns pillowy and the spice blooms. It is wonderful with a smear of salted butter, a spoonful of whipped cream, or a dollop of mascarpone, and it makes a superb base for a scoop of vanilla ice cream. For the full holiday experience, pair it with a mug of coffee, a glass of cold milk, or — naturally — a small glass of eggnog. For dessert, a warm slice with vanilla ice cream and a dusting of fresh nutmeg is hard to beat.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days — the crumb stays custardy and moist, and the nutmeg-sugar top stays crackly for the first day. After day 3, refrigerate for up to a week (the eggnog in the crumb means it keeps best cold) and bring slices back to room temperature before eating. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8-10 minutes or toast them lightly in a dry pan — the edges get deliciously crisp, the spice comes back to life, and the whole kitchen smells like Christmas morning.
