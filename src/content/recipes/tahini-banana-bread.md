Tahini and banana bread were made for each other. The sesame paste brings a toasty, nutty depth — a little like halva, a little like peanut butter — that balances the sweetness of ripe bananas, and a ribbon of honeyed tahini swirled through the crumb keeps every slice moist and marbled. Toasted sesame seeds on top bake into a gently crunchy crust, and a drizzle of tahini glaze finishes it like the best bakery loaf. It is less sweet than most banana breads, which makes it dangerously easy to eat with coffee at any hour.

This is the loaf to bake when you want banana bread to feel grown-up — for a slow weekend breakfast, a lunchbox that is not all sugar, or a cake tin at work that disappears before the kettle boils. It looks beautiful sliced, keeps beautifully, and tastes like something from a proper bakery.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

The tahini swirl is the star — it marbles through the crumb in pale, nutty ribbons so every slice has pockets of toasty sesame flavour. The tahini in the batter keeps the loaf extraordinarily moist without making it heavy, and the sesame-seed topping adds a light crunch that contrasts with the soft crumb. It is a one-bowl, 15-minute-hands-on bake that eats like something far fancier, and it is endlessly adaptable: add dark chocolate chips, fold in chopped dates, or serve it warm with a spoonful of honey.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black-skinned bananas are essential — they bring the sweetness and moisture this loaf needs. Mash until mostly smooth; a few small lumps add nice texture. Measure the mash: 1 1/4 cups is the target. Extra-wet bananas make a thin batter, and a thin batter lets the tahini swirl sink.

**Well-stirred tahini.** This is the ingredient that makes or breaks the loaf. Tahini separates in the jar — thick paste at the bottom, oil on top — so stir it thoroughly until it is completely smooth and pourable before measuring. Unstirred tahini measures as mostly paste and bakes up dry. Use a runny, good-quality tahini; the thick, grainy kind at the bottom of an old jar will not marble properly.

**Brown sugar and butter.** Brown sugar's molasses notes deepen the sesame flavour and keep the crumb moist. Melted butter blends easily into the banana-tahini mixture and gives a rich, tender crumb — just let it cool slightly so it doesn't scramble the eggs.

**The tahini-honey swirl.** Three tablespoons of tahini whisked with a tablespoon of honey makes a loose, pourable swirl that marbles beautifully through the batter. It is the same idea as a cinnamon swirl, but the honey keeps the sesame flavour from turning bitter and adds a floral note that pairs perfectly with banana.

**Toasted sesame seeds.** Buy them toasted, or toast raw seeds in a dry pan over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, shaking often, until golden and fragrant. They go on top before baking and turn gently crunchy in the oven — do not skip them, they are the first thing you taste.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The only technique that matters here is the stirring and the marbling. Start by stirring the tahini until it flows, then whisk the wet ingredients smooth in one bowl and the dry ingredients together in another. Fold the dry into the wet until just combined — a few lumps are fine; over-stirring develops gluten and bakes up tough instead of tender.

Pour half the batter into the lined pan, dollop half the tahini-honey swirl over it, add the remaining batter, then the rest of the swirl on top. Now marble: run a butter knife through the batter in a loose figure-eight, two or three passes only. That is the whole trick — gentle passes leave ribbons, enthusiastic stirring mixes the tahini away. Finish with the toasted sesame seeds scattered evenly over the surface.

Start checking at 55 minutes: a toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not raw batter. The top should be deeply golden — the sesame seeds darken faster than a plain loaf, so tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes if it is browning before the centre is done.

Cooling matters. The loaf needs a full 45 minutes on the rack before slicing, or the soft crumb will compress and the warm swirl will smear. Use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts for clean slices that show off the marbling. Glaze only once the loaf is completely cool, or the tahini glaze will slide straight off.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Stir the tahini until it pours.** If it will not loosen, warm the jar for 10 seconds in the microwave. A stiff, grainy tahini will not marble and will bake up dry and pasty in places.
- **Measure the mashed banana.** Three bananas of any size is not a measurement — 1 1/4 cups is. Too much banana makes a thin batter, and a thin batter lets the swirl sink to the bottom.
- **Marble gently.** Two or three figure-eight passes with a butter knife are plenty. More than that and the tahini disappears into the batter instead of leaving ribbons.
- **Toast your own sesame seeds** if you only have raw ones — 2–3 minutes in a dry pan, shaking often, until golden and fragrant. Raw seeds taste flat; toasted seeds taste like the whole point of the loaf.
- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. The tahini glaze gets sticky as it sets, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Don't slice warm.** The crumb needs time to set or it compresses under the knife. Forty-five minutes on the rack is the hardest part of this recipe — and the most important.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Dark chocolate tahini.** Fold in 3/4 cup dark chocolate chips with the dry ingredients. They melt into pockets that balance the sesame's savoury edge beautifully — this is the version to bake for chocolate lovers.

**Date and tahini.** Fold in 3/4 cup chopped Medjool dates with the dry ingredients and drizzle the cooled loaf with extra honey instead of glaze. The dates add chewy, caramel sweetness that pairs perfectly with tahini.

**Peanut butter tahini.** Replace half the tahini in the batter with smooth peanut butter for a nuttier, more familiar flavour — the swirl stays tahini and honey. Top with chopped roasted peanuts instead of sesame seeds.

**Tahini banana muffins.** Divide the batter between 12 lined muffin cups, spooning a little tahini-honey swirl into each as you fill them, and top each with sesame seeds. Bake at 350°F for 20–23 minutes. They are perfect for lunchboxes and bake sales.

**Orange tahini.** Add the zest of one orange to the wet ingredients and replace the honey in the swirl with orange blossom honey if you have it. The citrus lifts the sesame flavour and makes the loaf taste brighter and lighter.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm a slice for 10 seconds in the microwave so the tahini perfume wakes up and the crumb turns pillowy. It is wonderful with a smear of salted butter, a spoonful of honey, or a dollop of Greek yoghurt, and it makes a superb base for a scoop of vanilla or sesame ice cream. For the full café experience, pair it with a flat white or a mug of mint tea — the coffee's bitterness plays beautifully against the nutty sweetness. For dessert, a warm slice with a drizzle of honey and a pinch of flaky sea salt is hard to beat.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days — the sesame-seed top stays crunchy for the first day and softens slightly after that, while the crumb stays moist. After day 3, refrigerate for up to a week and bring slices back to room temperature before eating. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes or toast them lightly in a dry pan — the edges get deliciously crisp, the sesame aroma comes back, and the whole kitchen smells like a bakery.
