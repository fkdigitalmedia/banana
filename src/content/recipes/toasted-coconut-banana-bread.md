There is something about toasted coconut that turns an ordinary banana bread into a small holiday. The edges go golden and caramelised, the kitchen smells like a beachside bakery, and every slice has chewy, nutty flakes of coconut running through a soft, buttery crumb. This loaf leans fully into that: coconut oil and coconut milk in the batter, a full cup of toasted coconut folded in, and more scattered over the top so it bakes into a crisp, golden crust.

It is still the same forgiving, one-bowl banana bread you know — no mixer, no creaming, fifteen minutes of actual work — just dressed up for somewhere warmer.

### Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice has coconut in three places: in the batter, folded through the crumb, and crisp on top. The coconut oil keeps the loaf moist for days without tasting greasy, and the brown sugar plus sweetened coconut caramelise together into a top that is almost like a macaroon crust. It uses up three spotty bananas, it travels well, and it is just as good toasted for breakfast as it is warm from the oven with a cup of coffee.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black skins mean maximum sweetness and a soft, jammy crumb. Mash them until mostly smooth — a few small lumps are fine and add texture.

**Toasted coconut — the star.** Toasting is not optional garnish here; it transforms coconut from soft and sweet into nutty, golden, and fragrant. Toast it yourself rather than buying pre-toasted flakes, which are often unevenly browned and stale. Sweetened shredded coconut gives the best caramelised top.

**Butter + coconut oil.** Butter brings flavour; coconut oil brings moisture that lasts. Used together, you get both — a loaf that tastes buttery on day one and is still soft on day three. If you only have one, use all butter (for flavour) or all coconut oil (for a stronger tropical note).

**Sour cream + coconut milk.** The sour cream keeps the crumb tender, while the coconut milk thins the batter just enough and deepens the coconut flavour without making it heavy. Shake or stir the tin well before measuring — the cream and water separate in the tin.

**Coconut extract.** Optional, but half a teaspoon makes the coconut flavour read clearly even after baking, when much of the fresh coconut aroma bakes off. Skip it if you prefer a subtler loaf.

**Baking soda.** The only leavener here. It reacts with the acidic bananas and sour cream for lift, so check yours is not expired — a pinch dropped in vinegar should fizz vigorously.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

Toast the coconut first, while the oven preheats, and watch it like a hawk. Spread it in a thin, even layer — piled-up coconut steams instead of toasting — and stir halfway so the edges do not burn while the middle stays pale. You want patchy gold, not uniform brown; it will darken a little more on top of the loaf in the oven.

Do not overmix once the flour goes in. Fold the dry ingredients in two additions, alternating with the sour cream and coconut milk, and stop as soon as no dry streaks remain. Overmixed batter develops gluten and bakes up tough and dense instead of tender — and a tough crumb is the one thing that can ruin an otherwise perfect banana bread.

The topping is the part that needs attention in the oven. Sweetened coconut browns fast, so start checking the colour at 40 minutes. Once the top is deep golden, tent loosely with foil and let the center finish. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter — and remember that a melted coconut flake on the toothpick does not count as raw batter.

### Expert Tips

- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. The caramelised coconut top can stick to the pan edges, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Room temperature eggs** blend into the batter smoothly instead of seizing the melted butter and coconut oil. Quick fix: put the eggs in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Cool before slicing.** 15 minutes in the pan, then fully on a rack. Slicing too early compresses the soft crumb and the coconut crust sheds.
- **For clean slices,** use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts — the coconut flakes drag otherwise.
- **Toast slices on day two and three.** A few minutes in a toaster or under the grill re-crispens the coconut top and re-melts the crumb.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Macadamia coconut.** Fold in 1/2 cup of toasted, roughly chopped macadamia nuts with the coconut — the classic tropical pairing, buttery and crunchy.

**Pineapple coconut.** Fold in 1/2 cup of well-drained crushed pineapple (squeeze it dry in a tea towel first) and reduce the coconut milk to 2 tablespoons, so the batter does not get too wet. It bakes up like a pina colada loaf.

**White chocolate coconut.** Fold in 3/4 cup of white chocolate chips. They melt into sweet pockets that pair beautifully with the toasted coconut.

**Lime coconut.** Add 1 tablespoon of finely grated lime zest to the batter and a squeeze of lime over the warm loaf. The citrus lifts the richness and makes the coconut taste brighter.

**Chocolate-dipped slices.** Once cooled, dip half of each slice in melted dark chocolate and let it set. It turns leftover banana bread into something that looks straight out of a bakery case.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm them for 10 seconds in the microwave — just enough to soften the crumb without melting the crust. It is wonderful with salted butter, a smear of cream cheese, or a drizzle of warm honey. For breakfast, pair with black coffee or a coconut latte; for dessert, serve with vanilla ice cream and a spoonful of toasted coconut over the top.

### Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days, or refrigerate for up to a week. The coconut topping is crispest on day one — toast slices lightly on later days to bring the crunch back. To freeze, wrap the cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature, then refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8-10 minutes or toast them lightly — the edges get deliciously crisp and the coconut re-toasts.
