There's regular banana bread, and then there's banana bread with warm, caramelised Biscoff cookie butter marbled through every slice and a crunchy biscuit crumble on top. This is the second one. The batter itself is soft and buttery thanks to sour cream and brown sugar, but the swirl is what makes people close their eyes on the first bite — spiced, caramelly, and melting right into the crumb as it bakes.

It's also one of the easiest bakes in my kitchen. One bowl for the wet ingredients, one for the dry, a small dish for the crumble, and 15 minutes of actual work. The oven does the rest.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice has a visible cookie butter swirl — no pale, boring banana bread here. The sour cream keeps the crumb tender and moist for days, the brown sugar gives a gentle caramel depth, and the Biscoff crumble bakes into a crisp, spiced crust on top. It uses up three spotty bananas, comes together without a mixer, and the whole house smells like a bakery while it bakes.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** This is non-negotiable for the best flavour. Heavily speckled or black skins mean maximum natural sweetness, so you don't need extra sugar. Mash them until mostly smooth — a few small lumps are fine and add nice texture.

**Biscoff cookie butter.** A spread made from caramelised speculoos biscuits — sweet, warmly spiced, and a little like gingerbread. Smooth or crunchy both work. Warm it for 15–20 seconds so it pours in a ribbon, but don't overheat it: too runny and it sinks to the bottom of the loaf.

**Brown sugar (in two places).** Packed light brown sugar sweetens the batter with a molasses warmth, and mixed into the crumble it helps the biscuit crumbs bake into a crunchy topping. Dark brown sugar gives a deeper caramel note if that's what you have.

**Sour cream.** The secret to a tender, bakery-style crumb. Its acidity relaxes the gluten and adds moisture that keeps the loaf soft for days. Full-fat plain Greek yogurt works as a substitute.

**Melted butter.** Used in both the batter and the crumble. Melting (rather than creaming) gives a denser, moister crumb — exactly what you want in a quick bread.

**Ground cinnamon.** Just half a teaspoon in the batter — it echoes the warm spice in the Biscoff without overpowering the banana. Make sure your cinnamon is fresh; ground cinnamon loses punch after about a year.

**Baking soda.** The only leavener here. It reacts with the acidic bananas and sour cream for lift, so make sure yours isn't expired — test it by dropping a pinch in vinegar; it should fizz vigorously.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The swirl is the only part that needs a little technique, and it's simpler than it looks. The key is layering: half the batter, half the warmed cookie butter, remaining batter, remaining cookie butter. Then one confident S-shaped drag of a butter knife through the pan, going only about an inch deep. You're marbling, not stirring — two or three passes is plenty. Over-swirling muddies the ribbon into the batter instead of leaving distinct layers.

Don't overmix once the flour goes in. Stir just until no dry streaks remain. A few lumps are fine; overmixed batter develops gluten and bakes up tough and dense instead of tender.

The crumble goes on last and bakes into a crisp, golden crust — sprinkle it evenly so no patch of batter is bare. Start checking at 55 minutes. The toothpick test is slightly tricky here because the melted cookie butter can look wet — look for moist crumbs clinging to the toothpick rather than raw batter. If the crumble browns too fast, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. The caramelised swirl can stick, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Room temperature egg** blends into the batter smoothly instead of seizing the melted butter. Quick fix: put the egg in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Cool before slicing.** 15 minutes in the pan, then fully on a rack. Slicing too early compresses the soft crumb and the swirl smears.
- **For clean slices,** use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts.
- **Crush the biscuits by hand** in a zip bag — you want a mix of fine crumbs and pea-sized pieces for the best crunch, not uniform dust.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Chocolate chip Biscoff.** Fold 1/2 cup of dark chocolate chips into the batter — chocolate and cookie butter are a natural pair.

**Banana Biscoff muffins.** Divide the batter between 12 lined muffin cups, swirl a teaspoon of cookie butter into each, and sprinkle with the crumble. Bake at 350°F for 20–23 minutes.

**Peanut butter swirl.** Swap the cookie butter for smooth peanut butter and add a handful of chopped roasted peanuts to the crumble for a salty-sweet twist.

**Extra spiced.** Add 1/4 teaspoon of ground ginger and a pinch of nutmeg to the batter to lean into the speculoos spice.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm them for 10 seconds in the microwave to re-melt the cookie butter ribbon. It's wonderful with salted butter, a drizzle of warm cookie butter, or a scoop of vanilla ice cream for dessert. For breakfast, pair with hot coffee — the caramelised spice and coffee are made for each other.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days; the crumble stays crispest on day one, so eat the end slices first if you love the crunch. After day 3, move it to the fridge for up to a week. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes — the edges get deliciously crisp and the topping re-crisps nicely.
