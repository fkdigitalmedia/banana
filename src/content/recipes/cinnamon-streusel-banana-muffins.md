instructions:
  - "Heat oven to 375°F (190°C). Line a 12-cup standard muffin tin with paper liners, or grease it well."
  - "Make the streusel first: in a small bowl, stir together the flour, brown sugar, and cinnamon. Rub in the cold butter with your fingertips until the mixture forms pea-sized crumbs and clumps. Stir in the chopped nuts, if using. Set aside in the fridge while you mix the batter."
  - "In a large bowl, whisk the mashed bananas, melted butter, and brown sugar until smooth. Whisk in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla and sour cream."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, and cinnamon. Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture until just combined — stop as soon as no dry streaks remain. A few small lumps are fine."
  - "Divide the batter evenly among the 12 muffin cups, filling each about three-quarters full. Pile a generous tablespoon of streusel on top of each muffin and press it down very lightly so it sticks."
  - "Bake 20–22 minutes, until the tops are golden, the streusel is crisp, and a toothpick inserted in the center of a muffin comes out with a few moist crumbs. Cool 5 minutes in the tin, then transfer to a wire rack."

Banana bread is wonderful, but sometimes you want the same warm, banana-scented comfort in a single-serving package — no slicing, no waiting for a loaf to cool, just a muffin you can grab warm from the tin. These cinnamon streusel banana muffins are exactly that, with one crucial upgrade: a thick, buttery cinnamon crumble baked right on top. The muffin underneath is soft, moist, and deeply banana-flavored; the top is golden, crunchy, and just sweet enough to make you reach for a second one.

They're also the fastest banana bake in my kitchen. One bowl for the batter, one small bowl for the streusel, 15 minutes of prep, and the oven does the rest in about 20 minutes. Perfect for using up three spotty bananas on a busy morning.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every muffin gets its own crunchy cinnamon-sugar crown — no fighting over the crusty end slice like with a loaf. The crumb stays tender and moist for days thanks to sour cream and melted butter, and the recipe scales easily: double it for a crowd, or bake one batch and freeze half. They're equally at home in a lunchbox, on a brunch table, or warmed up for an afternoon snack with coffee.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** This is the single most important ingredient. Heavily speckled or black skins mean maximum natural sweetness and banana flavor, so the muffins need less added sugar. Mash until mostly smooth — a few small lumps add lovely texture.

**Brown sugar (in two places).** Packed brown sugar sweetens the batter with a gentle molasses warmth, and mixed with flour, cinnamon, and cold butter it becomes the crumbly streusel. Light or dark brown sugar both work; dark gives a deeper caramel note.

**Sour cream.** The secret to a tender, bakery-style crumb. Its acidity relaxes the gluten and adds moisture that keeps the muffins soft for days. Full-fat plain Greek yogurt is an equal substitute.

**Melted butter in the batter, cold butter in the streusel.** This contrast matters. Melted butter gives a dense, moist crumb; cold butter rubbed into the streusel ingredients stays in little pieces that bake into crisp, distinct crumbs instead of melting into a paste.

**Baking soda + baking powder.** The soda reacts with the acidic bananas and sour cream for lift, while the powder gives extra rise for a nicely domed muffin top. Make sure both are fresh — test baking soda by dropping a pinch in vinegar; it should fizz vigorously.

**Ground cinnamon.** One teaspoon in the batter and another in the streusel. The spice is the thread that ties the soft banana crumb to the crunchy topping. Use fresh cinnamon if you can — ground cinnamon loses punch after about a year.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

Start with the streusel, not the batter. Rubbing cold butter into flour, sugar, and cinnamon takes a minute, and chilling the finished streusel while you mix the batter keeps the butter firm so it bakes up crisp. If the streusel feels pasty, your butter was too warm — pop the bowl in the fridge for 10 minutes.

For the batter, the order is simple: wet ingredients first (bananas, melted butter, sugar, eggs, vanilla, sour cream), then fold in the whisked dry ingredients just until combined. Stop the moment the last dry streak disappears. A few lumps are fine and actually desirable — they disappear in the oven.

![Golden streusel topping close-up on a warm banana muffin](/images/recipes/cinnamon-streusel-banana-muffins/pin-3.webp)

Fill the cups three-quarters full — fuller and the batter rises over the streusel, burying it; less full and you get squat muffins with a thin topping. Spoon the streusel on generously and press it down very lightly, just enough to anchor it. Bake at a fairly hot 375°F so the muffins spring up quickly and the streusel crisps before the crumb dries out. Start checking at 20 minutes: the tops should be golden and a toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Line the tin with paper liners** for easy removal — the sugary streusel can stick to an unlined tin. If you grease instead, grease generously.
- **Room temperature eggs** blend smoothly into the batter instead of seizing the melted butter. Quick fix: put the eggs in a bowl of warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Don't overbake.** Muffins dry out fast. Pull them when the toothpick shows moist crumbs; carryover heat finishes the job.
- **Cool 5 minutes in the tin, then move to a rack.** Leaving them in the hot tin steams the bottoms and softens the streusel.
- **For tall bakery-style domes,** fill the cups a touch fuller (just under the rim) and make sure your oven is fully preheated before the tin goes in.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Chocolate chip.** Fold 3/4 cup of semi-sweet chocolate chips into the batter — chocolate and cinnamon are a wonderful pairing.

**Nutty crunch.** The streusel already welcomes chopped pecans or walnuts; you can also fold 1/2 cup of toasted chopped nuts into the batter itself.

**Apple cinnamon.** Fold 1 cup of finely diced apple into the batter for a soft, jammy surprise in every bite.

**Cream cheese center.** Drop a teaspoon of sweetened cream cheese into the middle of each muffin cup before adding the streusel — a tangy core against the sweet crumb.

**Maple glaze.** Once cool, drizzle with a simple glaze of powdered sugar and maple syrup for a breakfast-pastry finish.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve warm from the oven, or refresh day-old muffins in a 300°F oven for 5–8 minutes to re-crisp the streusel. A smear of salted butter melting into a warm muffin is hard to beat. For brunch, pair with hot coffee or chai — the cinnamon in the topping loves both. They also pack beautifully for lunchboxes and bake sales, since each muffin is its own neat serving.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days; a paper towel in the container absorbs excess moisture and keeps the streusel crisp. For longer storage, freeze individually wrapped muffins for up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature for about an hour, or warm from frozen in a 300°F oven for 10–12 minutes. Avoid the microwave for reheating if you can — it softens the streusel. If you must, 10–15 seconds is enough.
