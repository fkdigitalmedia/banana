There's banana bread, and then there's banana bread with a shattering, caramelised crumb topping. This streusel-topped loaf is the second one, and it's the reason my loaf pans never stay clean for long. Underneath the golden streusel sits a deeply moist banana crumb — brown sugar, melted butter, and a spoonful of Greek yogurt make sure of that. But the topping is the star: buttery, cinnamon-flecked crumbs that stay crisp for days and turn every slice into a texture showdown between soft and crunchy.

It looks like it came from a bakery window, but the method is pure weekday baking: one bowl for the batter, one small bowl for the streusel, fifteen minutes of hands-on work. Make the streusel first and chill it while you mix the batter — that's the entire secret.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

The streusel is what elevates this from everyday banana bread to something you'd serve to guests. It bakes into a golden, buttery crust that crunches under your knife while the crumb underneath stays soft and tender — that contrast is the whole recipe. The batter itself is no mixer required, uses just three bananas, and the cinnamon in both batter and topping gives every bite a warm, bakery-spiced flavour. It also photographs beautifully, if you're the sort who eats with their phone first.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** The darker and spottier, the better. Ripe bananas mash easily, bring natural sweetness so you can lean less on refined sugar, and give the crumb its signature banana depth. Measure after mashing — you want about 1 1/4 cups.

**Brown sugar (in batter and streusel).** Packed brown sugar sweetens the batter with a molasses warmth, and in the streusel it melts and re-crystallises into those crisp caramelised edges. Dark brown sugar works too and makes the topping even more toffee-like.

**Cold butter for the streusel.** This is the critical detail. Warm or melted butter blends into the sugar and flour into a paste that melts flat in the oven. Cold butter, rubbed in and kept cold until it bakes, stays in distinct nuggets that crisp up. Cube it straight from the fridge, rub it in with cold fingertips, and chill the bowl of crumbs while you mix the batter.

**Greek yogurt.** The moisture insurance policy. A quarter cup keeps the crumb tender and springy for days without making the batter thin. Full-fat plain sour cream is an equally good choice.

**Ground cinnamon.** Half a teaspoon in the batter, half a teaspoon in the streusel. It reads as warm spice, not cinnamon-bread — just enough to frame the banana.

**Baking soda.** The only leavener. It reacts with the acidic bananas and yogurt for lift, so check yours is fresh: a pinch dropped in vinegar should fizz hard.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The whole recipe hinges on one piece of technique — the streusel — and it's just fingertip work. Whisk the dry ingredients, add cold butter cubes, and rub until you have a mixture of pea-sized crumbs and larger clumps. Don't aim for uniformity; the mix of sizes is what makes an interesting, craggy topping. Then refrigerate it. A cold streusel hits the hot oven and firms up before the butter can melt out.

![Streusel-topped banana bread loaf with golden crumb topping](/images/recipes/streusel-topped-banana-bread/pin-1.webp)

The batter is deliberately thick — it needs to be, because the streusel sits on top of it for a full hour in the oven. Fold the flour in gently and stop the moment the last dry streak disappears. Overmixing develops gluten and bakes up tough, which is a shame in a loaf this good.

Scatter the chilled streusel evenly over the batter without pressing it down. Big clumps are fine — they'll be the crunchiest bits. Start checking at 55 minutes: a toothpick in the centre should come out with moist crumbs (not wet batter), and the topping should be deep golden and feel crisp when you tap it. If it's browning fast but the centre isn't done, tent with foil and give it the last 10–15 minutes.

Cool 15 minutes in the pan, then lift the loaf out on its parchment sling. Wait until it's fully cool before slicing — the streusel sets and crisps as it cools, and cutting early makes it slide.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Chill the streusel, always.** Make it first, fridge it while you work. Ten minutes of chilling is the difference between a distinct crumb topping and a melted-on smear.
- **Don't press the streusel down.** Scattering keeps air around the crumbs, which is what lets them crisp. Pressing fuses them to the batter.
- **Tent with foil if needed.** Streusel browns faster than the batter bakes through. A loose foil tent for the last 15 minutes keeps the top golden instead of scorched.
- **Use a serrated knife.** A straight chef's knife drags and crushes the streusel; a gentle sawing motion with a bread knife keeps every slice picture-perfect.
- **Parchment sling is mandatory.** Between the caramelised sugar and the crumbly top, this loaf does not want to come out of an unlined pan in one piece.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Toasted pecan streusel.** Stir 1/3 cup chopped toasted pecans into the crumbs before scattering — pecan and brown sugar is an unbeatable combination.

**Oat streusel.** Replace 2 tablespoons of the streusel flour with old-fashioned rolled oats for a chewier, granola-like crunch on top.

**Chai-spiced.** Swap the cinnamon in both batter and streusel for a chai spice blend (cardamom, ginger, clove) for a more complex warming flavour.

**Chocolate chip batter.** Fold 1/2 cup of dark chocolate chips into the batter — chocolate, banana, and caramelised streusel is a serious combination.

**Apple streusel banana bread.** Fold 1 cup of finely diced apple into the batter; it bakes up soft and jammy under the crunchy top.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature with the streusel side up, obviously. It's wonderful with salted butter — the salt against the sweet crumbly top is the move — or a smear of cream cheese. For dessert, warm a slice for 10 seconds in the microwave and add a scoop of vanilla ice cream; the warm-cold contrast with the crunch is excellent. At breakfast it pairs beautifully with strong coffee, which cuts through the sweetness of the caramelised topping.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days — but wrap it loosely or store cut-side down so condensation doesn't soften the streusel. Refrigerated, it keeps up to a week. For freezing, wrap the cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap then foil; freeze up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature, then refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes — the heat re-crisps the streusel beautifully. Toasting a slice is also fantastic: the topping gets extra caramelised edges.
