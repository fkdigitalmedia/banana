instructions:
  - "Heat oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x5-inch loaf pan and line with parchment, leaving an overhang on the long sides for easy lifting."
  - "In a large bowl, whisk the mashed bananas, melted butter, brown sugar, egg, vanilla, and sour cream until smooth and well combined."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, salt, and cocoa powder (if using). Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture until just combined — a few small lumps are fine. Don't overmix."
  - "Warm the Nutella briefly so it pours: microwave it in 10-second bursts, or set the jar in a bowl of warm water for 5 minutes. It should be loose and drizzle-able, not stiff."
  - "Pour half the batter into the prepared pan. Dollop half the warmed Nutella over the batter in spoonfuls. Add the remaining batter, then dollop the rest of the Nutella on top."

There's regular banana bread, and then there's banana bread with molten chocolate-hazelnut ribbons running through every single slice. This is the second one. The batter itself is rich and buttery thanks to sour cream and brown sugar, but the Nutella is what makes people close their eyes on the first bite — warm, gooey hazelnut chocolate that melts right into the crumb as it bakes.

It's also one of the most forgiving bakes in my kitchen. One bowl for the wet ingredients, one for the dry, a jar of Nutella, and 15 minutes of actual work. The oven does the rest.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice has a visible chocolate-hazelnut swirl — no pale, boring banana bread here. The sour cream keeps the crumb tender and moist for days, and the Nutella adds a rich chocolate depth without any extra measuring or melting of chocolate. It uses up three spotty bananas, comes together without a mixer, and the whole house smells like a bakery while it bakes.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** This is non-negotiable for the best flavor. Heavily speckled or black skins mean maximum natural sweetness, so you don't need extra sugar. Mash them until mostly smooth — a few small lumps are fine and add nice texture.

**Nutella.** Warm it before swirling — cold Nutella is stiff and sinks in clumps instead of marbling. A quick warm-up in the microwave or a warm-water bath makes it drizzle beautifully. Measure generously; the swirl is the whole point.

**Brown sugar.** Packed brown sugar sweetens the batter with a molasses warmth that pairs perfectly with chocolate and hazelnut. Light or dark brown sugar both work; dark gives a deeper caramel note.

**Sour cream.** The secret to a tender, bakery-style crumb. Its acidity relaxes the gluten and adds moisture that keeps the loaf soft for days. Full-fat plain Greek yogurt works as a substitute.

**Melted butter.** Melting (rather than creaming) gives a denser, moister crumb — exactly what you want in a quick bread.

**Cocoa powder (optional).** A single tablespoon deepens the chocolate colour of the crumb so the pale banana batter doesn't wash out the swirl visually. Skip it if you prefer a lighter, golden crumb.

**Baking soda.** The only leavener here. It reacts with the acidic bananas and sour cream for lift, so make sure yours isn't expired — test it by dropping a pinch in vinegar; it should fizz vigorously.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The swirl is the only part that needs a little technique, and it's simpler than it looks. The key is layering: half the batter, half the warmed Nutella dolloped in spoonfuls, remaining batter, remaining Nutella. Then one confident S-shaped drag of a butter knife through the pan, going only about an inch deep. You're marbling, not stirring — two or three passes is plenty. Over-swirling muddies the ribbon into the batter instead of leaving distinct chocolate layers.

Don't overmix once the flour goes in. Stir just until no dry streaks remain. A few lumps are fine; overmixed batter develops gluten and bakes up tough and dense instead of tender.

Start checking at 55 minutes. The toothpick test is slightly tricky here because melted Nutella can look wet — look for moist crumbs clinging to the toothpick rather than raw batter. If the top is browning too fast, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. The caramelized Nutella can stick, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Warm the Nutella first.** Cold spread sinks; warm, drizzle-able spread marbles. Ten seconds in the microwave is usually enough.
- **Room temperature egg** blends into the batter smoothly instead of seizing the melted butter. Quick fix: put the egg in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Cool before slicing.** 15 minutes in the pan, then fully on a rack. Slicing too early compresses the soft crumb and the Nutella smears.
- **For clean slices,** use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Toasted hazelnuts.** Fold 1/2 cup of chopped toasted hazelnuts into the batter — they echo the Nutella and add a lovely crunch against the soft, gooey swirl.

**Peanut butter swirl.** Replace half the Nutella with creamy peanut butter for a chocolate-peanut butter marbled loaf.

**Espresso kick.** Add 1 teaspoon of instant espresso powder to the dry ingredients — it deepens the chocolate flavor without tasting like coffee.

**Chocolate chips.** Fold 1/2 cup of dark chocolate chips into the batter for extra pockets of melted chocolate.

**Streusel top.** Sprinkle a brown sugar, flour, and butter streusel over the top before baking for a crunchy, caramelized crust.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm them for 10 seconds in the microwave to re-melt the Nutella ribbons. It's wonderful with a smear of extra Nutella, a dollop of whipped cream, or a scoop of vanilla ice cream for dessert. For breakfast, pair with hot coffee — the chocolate, hazelnut, and coffee are made for each other.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. The sour cream keeps it moist, but after day 3 move it to the fridge for up to a week. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes or toast them lightly — the edges get deliciously crisp and the Nutella goes gooey again.
