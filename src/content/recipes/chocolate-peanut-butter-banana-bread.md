If peanut butter cups and banana bread had a baby, it would be this loaf. A dark, fudgy chocolate crumb, a creamy peanut butter ribbon running through the middle, and melted chocolate chips in every bite — it's the kind of banana bread that disappears before it has properly cooled, and nobody in the house will believe it started with three spotty bananas.

The magic is in the balance. The cocoa gives the crumb a deep, almost brownie-like richness, the banana keeps it soft and moist, and the peanut butter swirl adds that salty-sweet creaminess that makes one slice never quite enough. It's a weekend bake, a lunchbox hero, and a perfectly respectable dessert all at once.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice has three things going on at once: fudgy chocolate crumb, melted chocolate chips, and a visible peanut butter swirl. The sour cream keeps it tender and moist for days, the brown sugar adds a gentle caramel depth, and the whole thing comes together in one bowl plus one for the dry ingredients — no mixer required. It uses up your ripest bananas and it freezes beautifully, so you can bake once and snack all week.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Non-negotiable for the best flavor. Heavily speckled or black skins mean maximum natural sweetness and a smooth mash — important in a chocolate batter, where the banana flavor has to hold its own against the cocoa. Mash until mostly smooth; a few small lumps are fine.

**Dutch-process cocoa powder.** Dutch-process cocoa is darker, smoother, and less acidic than natural cocoa, which gives this loaf its brownie-like crumb. Natural cocoa works too, but the loaf will taste a little sharper and lighter in colour. Sift it with the flour — cocoa clumps easily and nobody wants a dry pocket of it in their slice.

**Creamy peanut butter.** Use a regular creamy peanut butter for the swirl — it holds its shape and swirls cleanly. Natural peanut butter separates and can turn the swirl greasy, so if you use it, stir it very well first and warm it only until just pourable.

**Brown sugar.** Packed brown sugar sweetens the batter with a molasses warmth that pairs beautifully with both chocolate and peanut butter. Dark brown sugar gives an even deeper caramel note.

**Dark chocolate chips.** Use 1 cup, divided — most folded into the batter, the rest scattered on top so the finished loaf looks as good as it tastes. Semi-sweet chips work; milk chocolate makes it sweeter and softer. Chop a bar if you like bigger pools of melted chocolate.

**Sour cream.** The secret to a tender, bakery-style crumb. Its acidity relaxes the gluten and adds moisture that keeps the loaf soft for days. Full-fat plain Greek yogurt is a fine substitute.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The swirl is the only part that needs a little technique, and it's simpler than it looks. Warm the peanut butter just until it pours — 15 to 20 seconds in the microwave is usually enough. Hot, runny peanut butter sinks; barely-warm peanut butter stays put.

Layer the batter: half in the pan, half the peanut butter dolloped on top, remaining batter, remaining peanut butter. Then one confident S-shaped drag of a butter knife through the pan, going only about an inch deep. You're marbling, not stirring — two or three passes is plenty. Over-swirling muddies the ribbon into the batter instead of leaving distinct swirls.

Don't overmix once the flour and cocoa go in. Stir just until no dry streaks remain. A few lumps are fine; overmixed batter develops gluten and bakes up tough and dense instead of tender and fudgy.

Start checking at 55 minutes. The toothpick test is slightly tricky here because melted chocolate and peanut butter can look wet — look for moist crumbs clinging to the toothpick rather than raw batter. If the top is browning too fast, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. The chocolate and peanut butter can stick, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Room temperature eggs** blend into the batter smoothly instead of seizing the melted butter. Quick fix: put the eggs in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Cool before slicing.** 15 minutes in the pan, then fully on a rack. Slicing too early compresses the soft crumb and the peanut butter swirl smears instead of holding its ribbon.
- **For clean slices,** use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts — the melted chocolate will thank you.
- **Toast the peanuts** for the topping in a dry pan for 2–3 minutes until fragrant. It takes seconds and doubles the flavour.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Chocolate peanut butter muffins.** Divide the batter among 12 lined muffin cups, dollop peanut butter on each, swirl with a toothpick, and bake 20–23 minutes at 350°F. Perfect for lunchboxes.

**Extra-crunchy top.** Swap the chopped peanuts for a handful of crushed pretzels scattered over the batter before baking — salty, crunchy, and brilliant against the fudgy crumb.

**Banana-peanut butter cup loaf.** Press a few halved mini peanut butter cups into the top of the batter before baking. They melt into gooey pockets.

**Espresso kick.** Add 1 teaspoon of instant espresso powder to the dry ingredients. You won't taste coffee — it just makes the chocolate taste more chocolatey.

**Nut-free version.** Swap the peanut butter for sunflower seed butter and skip the peanut topping. The swirl still works, with a slightly earthier flavour.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm them for 15 seconds in the microwave so the chocolate chips go molten again. It's wonderful with a cold glass of milk, a smear of extra peanut butter, or a scoop of vanilla ice cream for dessert. For breakfast, pair with hot coffee — chocolate, peanut butter, and coffee are made for each other. A drizzle of warm chocolate sauce turns it into a proper pudding.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. After day 3, move it to the fridge for up to a week — the peanut butter swirl firms up when cold, so let slices come back to room temperature or warm them for 15 seconds in the microwave. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes or toast them lightly — the edges get deliciously crisp and the chocolate goes soft again.
