Chocolate and peppermint are the flavour of December — and folded into a banana bread, they make a loaf that is fudgy, fragrant and impossible to leave alone. The bananas keep the crumb deeply moist, the cocoa makes it dark and rich, and the peppermint lifts the whole thing with a cool, clean finish. A scattering of crushed candy canes on top adds crunch, colour and a little festive sparkle. It tastes like a holiday chocolate bar in loaf form, and it fills the kitchen with the smell of warm chocolate and mint while it bakes.

This is the loaf to bake in late November and December, when the candy canes appear in the shops and the bananas on the counter have gone properly speckled. It is wonderful for a holiday breakfast table, a gift wrapped in parchment and ribbon, or a quiet slice with a mug of coffee on a cold evening.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

The chocolate is the real deal here — Dutch-process cocoa plus a full cup of dark chocolate chips makes a loaf that is genuinely fudgy, not just chocolate-flavoured. The sour cream keeps the crumb tender and moist for days, and it deepens the chocolate flavour the way a pinch of salt deepens caramel. The peppermint is measured carefully: one teaspoon gives a clear, cool mint note that lifts the chocolate without tasting like toothpaste. And the crushed candy cane topping is the finishing touch — crunchy, sparkling and festive, it turns an everyday banana bread into something that belongs on a holiday table. It is a one-bowl, 20-minute-hands-on bake that tastes like you spent all afternoon on it.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black-skinned bananas are essential — they bring the sweetness and moisture this loaf needs. Mash until mostly smooth; a few small lumps add nice texture. Measure the mash: 1 1/4 cups is the target. Too much banana makes a wet batter, and a wet batter bakes up dense instead of fudgy.

**Dutch-process cocoa powder.** This is what makes the loaf dark and intensely chocolatey. Dutch-process cocoa is smoother and less acidic than natural cocoa, and it gives the crumb its deep, almost black colour. Sift it before measuring — cocoa clumps never dissolve once the batter is mixed, and they bake into bitter pockets.

**Peppermint extract.** The cool mint note that makes this loaf festive. Peppermint extract is much stronger than vanilla, so measure it carefully — one teaspoon is exactly right. Do not substitute mint extract, which has a greener, more herbal flavour; peppermint is the classic chocolate pairing.

**Sour cream.** The secret to a tender, long-lasting crumb. It adds moisture and a gentle tang that deepens the chocolate flavour and keeps the loaf moist for days. Full-fat Greek yogurt is a fine substitute; low-fat versions work too, but the loaf will be a touch less rich.

**Dark chocolate chips.** A full cup, folded in and sprinkled on top. They melt into glossy pools and fudgy pockets throughout the crumb. Use good-quality dark chocolate chips — they are half the flavour of this loaf.

**Crushed candy canes.** The festive topping. Crush them in a zip-top bag with a rolling pin — you want a mix of fine shards and small pieces, not dust. Sprinkle them on while the loaf is still warm so they stick without melting.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

There is nothing complicated here, and that is the point. Start by heating the oven and lining the pan — parchment with an overhang on the long sides makes lifting the loaf out easy, and a fudgy loaf needs that help.

Whisk the wet ingredients smooth in one bowl: mashed bananas, melted butter, both sugars, eggs, peppermint extract and vanilla. It should look creamy and glossy, with the butter fully incorporated. In a separate bowl, whisk the dry ingredients together — flour, cocoa, baking soda, baking powder and salt. Sift the cocoa if it is lumpy; this is the one step worth not skipping.

Fold the dry into the wet in two additions, adding the sour cream with the second — this keeps the batter from overworking and keeps the cocoa evenly distributed. Stir until just combined; a few streaks of flour are fine, and over-stirring develops gluten and bakes up tough instead of tender. Fold in 3/4 cup of the chocolate chips, pour the batter into the pan, smooth the top, and sprinkle the remaining chips over the surface.

Start checking at 55 minutes: a toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs. Melted chocolate on the toothpick is fine — look for wet batter, not chocolate. The top should be deeply browned and crackly; if it is browning before the centre is done, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes.

Cooling matters. Let the loaf cool 15 minutes in the pan, then lift it out and sprinkle the crushed candy canes over the top while it is still warm — the warmth helps them stick. Cool a full hour on the rack before slicing, or the fudgy crumb will compress and the slices will smear. Slice with a sharp serrated knife so the candy doesn't drag.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Sift the cocoa.** Cocoa powder clumps stubbornly, and clumps never dissolve once the batter is mixed — they bake into bitter pockets. A quick sift takes ten seconds and saves the loaf.
- **Measure the peppermint carefully.** Peppermint extract is far stronger than vanilla. One teaspoon is the sweet spot; a heavy hand will overpower the chocolate and taste like toothpaste. Use a measuring spoon, not a free pour.
- **Measure the mashed banana.** Three bananas of any size is not a measurement — 1 1/4 cups is. Too much banana makes a wet batter, and a wet batter bakes up dense instead of fudgy.
- **Read the toothpick correctly.** Melted chocolate on the toothpick is not wet batter. Look for wet, raw batter clinging to the toothpick — a few moist crumbs means done. Chocolate smears mean keep baking only if you also see batter.
- **Add the candy canes warm, not hot.** Straight from the oven they melt into a sticky layer; fully cold and they fall off. Sprinkle them on just after lifting the loaf out of the pan, while it is warm to the touch.
- **Don't slice warm.** The fudgy crumb needs time to set or it compresses under the knife. An hour on the rack is the hardest part of this recipe — and the most important.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**White chocolate peppermint.** Swap half the dark chocolate chips for white chocolate chips. They melt into creamy, sweet pockets that play beautifully against the cool peppermint — this is the version to bake for people who find dark chocolate too intense.

**Peppermint mocha.** Add 1 tablespoon of instant espresso powder to the dry ingredients. Coffee deepens chocolate the way salt deepens caramel, and the mocha note makes this loaf taste like a holiday café drink.

**Double mint crunch.** Fold in 1/2 cup of crushed peppermint bark or chopped Andes mints with the chocolate chips. They melt into cool, creamy pockets and add an extra layer of mint.

**Nutty peppermint.** Fold in 1/2 cup of chopped toasted pecans or walnuts. They add crunch against the soft, fudgy crumb and a buttery note that grounds the mint. Toast them 7-8 minutes at 350°F first, until fragrant.

**Chocolate peppermint muffins.** Divide the batter between 12 lined muffin cups, sprinkle each with a few chocolate chips, and bake at 350°F for 20-24 minutes. Sprinkle the crushed candy canes on while the muffins are still warm — perfect for a holiday breakfast or a cookie exchange.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm a slice for 10 seconds in the microwave so the chocolate chips go soft and the crumb turns pillowy. It is wonderful with a smear of salted butter, a spoonful of whipped cream, or a dollop of mascarpone, and it makes a superb base for a scoop of vanilla or peppermint ice cream. For the full holiday experience, pair it with a mug of coffee, a glass of cold milk, or a cup of hot chocolate with a candy cane stirred in. For dessert, a warm slice with vanilla ice cream and a dusting of cocoa is hard to beat.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days — the crumb stays fudgy and moist, and the candy cane top stays crunchy for the first day (humidity will soften it after that, so slice what you need and keep the rest wrapped). Refrigerate for up to a week and bring slices back to room temperature before eating. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8-10 minutes or toast them lightly in a dry pan — the edges get deliciously crisp, the chocolate goes soft again, and the whole kitchen smells like warm chocolate and mint.
