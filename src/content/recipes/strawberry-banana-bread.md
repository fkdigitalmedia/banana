instructions:
  - "Heat oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x5-inch loaf pan and line with parchment, leaving an overhang on the long sides for easy lifting."
  - "In a large bowl, whisk the mashed bananas, melted butter, granulated sugar, brown sugar, egg, vanilla, and milk until smooth and well combined."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk 1 1/2 cups flour with the baking soda and salt. Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture until just combined — a few small lumps are fine. Don't overmix."
  - "Toss the diced strawberries with the extra tablespoon of flour so every piece is lightly coated (this stops them sinking). Fold them gently into the batter with no more than 4–5 strokes."

Strawberry season and banana bread season have one glorious thing in common: they both peak when you have fruit going soft on the counter. This loaf is what happens when those two worlds collide — a classic moist banana bread crumb, but every slice is studded with fresh strawberries that bake up jammy and leave little pink streaks through the crumb. It looks like something from a bakery window and tastes like early summer.

It's still an easy one-bowl-ish bake. The only real technique is tossing the berries in flour so they don't sink, and everything else is the banana bread you already know how to make. Fifteen minutes of work, an hour in the oven, and the house smells like a strawberry field.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

The strawberries do something magical here: they soften into jammy pockets that contrast beautifully with the tender banana crumb, and their slight tartness balances the sweetness instead of just adding more sugar. The brown sugar in the batter adds a caramel depth, the melted butter keeps everything rich, and the loaf slices cleanly into gorgeous pink-flecked pieces that photograph as well as they taste. It uses up both overripe bananas and the strawberries that are one day away from being too soft to eat fresh.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black-skinned bananas are sweetest and mash smoothest. They provide most of the flavour here, so the riper, the better. If yours aren't ready, bake them unpeeled at 300°F for 15–20 minutes until the skins blacken.

**Fresh strawberries.** Use berries that are ripe and sweet but still firm enough to dice — mushy berries collapse into mush and make the loaf wet. Hull and dice them into roughly 1/2-inch pieces: small enough to distribute evenly, big enough to stay juicy. About 250 g (1 1/2 cups diced) is the sweet spot; more than that and the structure suffers.

**The flour toss.** Don't skip tossing the diced berries in 1 tablespoon of flour before folding them in. It's the difference between berries suspended through every slice and a sad strawberry layer at the bottom of the pan.

**Brown + white sugar.** The granulated sugar gives clean sweetness; the brown sugar adds molasses warmth and extra moisture. This combination is what makes the crust bake up golden rather than pale.

**Melted butter.** Melting instead of creaming gives a denser, moister crumb — exactly right for a quick bread. Let it cool slightly before whisking so it doesn't cook the egg.

**Baking soda.** The only leavener. It reacts with the acidic bananas and strawberries for lift, so check yours is fresh — a pinch dropped in vinegar should fizz vigorously.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The whole game with this loaf is berry management. Dice the strawberries first, toss with flour, and set aside — once the flour hits the wet batter, the clock starts, and you don't want berries sitting and weeping while you fumble.

Whisk the wet ingredients until completely smooth, then fold the dry in just until the flour disappears. A few lumps are fine. Then add the floured berries and fold with a light hand — four or five strokes, no more. Every extra stroke develops gluten and risks crushing the berries, which releases juice and dyes the batter pink (pretty, but it weakens the crumb).

In the oven, this loaf needs the full time. The berries add moisture, so it can look done on top while the centre is still setting — trust the toothpick, and test in a crumb patch rather than spearing a berry. If the top is getting dark before the centre is done, a loose foil tent for the last 15 minutes solves it without steaming the crust.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Pat the berries dry** after dicing. Even fresh strawberries weep a little juice once cut, and that surface moisture is what makes slices soggy.
- **Line the pan with a parchment sling.** The jammy berry pockets can stick, and the overhang lets you lift the whole loaf out without breaking it.
- **Cool completely before slicing.** This matters more here than with plain banana bread — the warm, berry-rich crumb is fragile. Give it the full cool on a wire rack.
- **Use a serrated knife** and a gentle sawing motion for clean slices that don't squash the berries.
- **Don't overfill the pan.** The batter should fill no more than about two-thirds of the pan; this loaf rises less than plain banana bread because of the berry weight.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**White chocolate strawberry.** Fold in 1/2 cup white chocolate chips with the berries — the creamy sweetness against the tart berries is a bakery-case classic.

**Strawberry cream cheese swirl.** Beat 4 oz softened cream cheese with 2 tablespoons sugar and 1 egg yolk, dollop over half the batter, add the rest of the batter, and swirl lightly. Rich, tangy, and gorgeous sliced.

**Lemon strawberry.** Add 1 tablespoon of lemon zest to the wet ingredients. Lemon makes strawberries taste more like themselves, and the brightness is wonderful against the banana.

**Strawberry streusel.** Mix 1/3 cup flour, 1/3 cup brown sugar, and 3 tablespoons cold butter into crumbs and sprinkle over the batter before baking for a crunchy, caramelised top.

**Mixed berry.** Swap half the strawberries for blueberries or raspberries — just keep the total at 1 1/2 cups and toss them all in flour.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve at room temperature in thick slices — the berries are the star, so let them shine. A thin smear of salted butter or mascarpone takes it firmly into dessert territory, and a drizzle of honey doesn't hurt. For breakfast, it's perfect as-is with hot coffee or a cold glass of milk. If you're serving it to guests, warm the slices for 10 seconds in the microwave first: the berry pockets go jammy again and the whole thing smells incredible.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Because of the fresh fruit, this loaf has a shorter room-temperature life than plain banana bread: wrapped tightly or in an airtight container, it's at its best within 2 days. After that, refrigerate for up to 5 days and bring slices to room temperature (or warm them 10 seconds in the microwave) before serving — cold mutes the berry flavour.

For longer keeping, freeze it: wrap the cooled loaf or individual slices in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes or give them a quick toast — the edges crisp up beautifully and the berries warm through.
