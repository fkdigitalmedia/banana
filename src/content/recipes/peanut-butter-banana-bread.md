Peanut butter and banana is one of those flavor pairings that needs no introduction — but baked together in a loaf with a marbled peanut butter top and a whisper of flaky sea salt, it becomes something genuinely special. This bread is rich but not heavy: the peanut butter melts into the crumb and keeps it unbelievably moist, while the brown sugar and vanilla round out the roasted, nutty depth.

It's a one-bowl (well, almost) bake that takes 15 minutes of hands-on time. No mixer, no fussy techniques — just a swirl of warm peanut butter across the top that looks impressive with almost zero effort.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

The peanut butter does double duty here: half a cup folded into the batter for richness and moisture, plus a marbled swirl on top that bakes into a gorgeous, bakery-window finish. The flaky sea salt is the finishing touch that makes the nutty sweetness pop. It stays soft for days, toasts beautifully, and works as breakfast, snack, or dessert.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Black-spotted bananas are the sweetest and most flavorful — they mash smooth and keep the bread tender. If yours aren't ripe, bake them unpeeled at 300°F for 15–20 minutes until the skins blacken, then cool and mash.

**Creamy peanut butter.** A standard commercial creamy peanut butter (the no-stir kind) gives the smoothest, most reliable result — natural-style peanut butters can separate and make the crumb oily. Use creamy in the batter and for the swirl drizzle; crunchy is fine in the batter if you want more texture.

**Brown sugar.** Its molasses note deepens the peanut butter's roastiness in a way white sugar doesn't. Packed light brown sugar is perfect; dark brown sugar makes the loaf slightly darker and more caramel-like.

**Milk.** Just a quarter cup loosens the batter to the right consistency — the peanut butter makes it thicker than a standard banana bread. Any milk works, dairy or plant-based.

**Baking soda.** The only leavener, reacting with the acidic bananas for lift. Check that yours is fresh — drop a pinch in vinegar; it should fizz energetically.

**Flaky sea salt.** Sprinkled on top before baking, it melts slightly into the swirl and gives little salty hits against the sweet, nutty crumb. Don't skip it.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

Start by whisking the wet ingredients until the peanut butter is fully incorporated — you want a smooth, glossy mixture with no streaks of peanut butter hiding at the bottom of the bowl. It takes a little more elbow grease than plain banana bread batter, but it pays off in an even, tender crumb.

Fold in the flour mixture gently and stop the moment the last dry streak disappears. Overmixing develops gluten and turns this rich loaf dense and gummy instead of soft. The batter should be thick but spreadable — if it seems stiff, that's the peanut butter talking; a spoonful of milk will loosen it.

For the swirl, warm the peanut butter just until it flows (15–20 seconds in the microwave, stirred). Drizzle it in lines down the length of the loaf, then drag a butter knife in an S-pattern, only about an inch deep. You're marbling the surface, not stirring the batter — three or four confident passes is plenty. Over-swirling muddies the design.

Start checking at 55 minutes. Peanut butter browns faster than plain batter, so if the top is getting dark before the center is done, tent it loosely with foil for the last 10–15 minutes. The toothpick should come out with moist crumbs, not wet batter.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. The peanut butter swirl caramelizes at the edges and can stick.
- **Warm, pourable peanut butter** is the secret to a pretty swirl. Too cold and it clumps; too hot and it melts straight into the batter.
- **Cool before slicing.** 15 minutes in the pan, then fully on a rack. Peanut butter bread is extra soft when hot and will compress if you cut it too soon.
- **For clean slices,** use a serrated bread knife and wipe the blade between cuts.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Chocolate peanut butter.** Fold 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips into the batter — they melt into gooey pockets that are pure heaven against the salty swirl.

**Crunchy honey peanut.** Use crunchy peanut butter in the batter and swap the chopped peanuts for honey-roasted peanuts. Sweet-salty-crunchy in every bite.

**Banana peanut butter muffins.** Divide the batter among 12 lined muffin cups (fill 3/4 full), add a small dollop of peanut butter on each, swirl with a toothpick, and bake at 350°F for 20–22 minutes.

**Chocolate drizzle finish.** Once the loaf is fully cool, drizzle melted dark chocolate over the top for a dessert-worthy presentation.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm for 10 seconds in the microwave so the swirl goes soft and melty again. It's excellent with a glass of cold milk, hot coffee, or — for the full peanut butter experience — a smear of extra peanut butter on each slice. For dessert, top a warm slice with vanilla ice cream and a drizzle of honey.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. It keeps a day longer than most quick breads because the peanut butter's fats hold moisture. Refrigerate for up to a week if your kitchen is warm. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf or individual slices in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months — thaw at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes or pop them in the toaster; the edges get golden and crisp while the peanut butter swirl re-melts.
