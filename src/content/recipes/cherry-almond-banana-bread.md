  - "1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled"
  - "3/4 cup granulated sugar"
  - "1/4 cup brown sugar, packed"
  - "2 large eggs, room temperature"
  - "1 tsp vanilla extract"
  - "1/2 tsp almond extract"
  - "1 3/4 cups all-purpose flour"
  - "1 tsp baking soda"
  - "1/2 tsp baking powder"
  - "1/2 tsp salt"
  - "1/4 tsp ground cinnamon"
  - "1/2 cup sour cream"
  - "1 1/2 cups fresh or frozen cherries, pitted and halved (about 10 oz)"
  - "1 tbsp all-purpose flour, for tossing the cherries"
  - "3/4 cup sliced almonds, toasted, divided"
  - "For the topping: 2 tbsp sliced almonds"
  - "For the topping: 1 tbsp coarse sugar"
instructions:
  - "Heat oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x5-inch loaf pan and line with parchment, leaving an overhang on the long sides for easy lifting."
  - "Toast the sliced almonds in a dry skillet over medium heat for 2-3 minutes, shaking the pan often, until golden and fragrant. Set aside to cool completely."
  - "In a large bowl, whisk the mashed bananas, melted butter, granulated sugar, brown sugar, eggs, vanilla extract, and almond extract until smooth and well combined."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, and cinnamon. Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture until just combined — a few small lumps are fine. Fold in the sour cream until no streaks remain. Don't overmix."
  - "Toss the halved cherries with the tablespoon of flour so they are lightly coated — this keeps them from sinking to the bottom of the loaf. Gently fold the cherries and 1/2 cup of the toasted sliced almonds into the batter."
  - "Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top. Sprinkle over the remaining 1/4 cup of toasted almonds, plus the extra 2 tablespoons for the topping and the coarse sugar."
  - "Bake 55-65 minutes, until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs (a cherry will leave a wet red streak — test away from the fruit). If the top browns too fast, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes."
  - "Cool 15 minutes in the pan, then lift out using the parchment sling and cool completely on a wire rack before slicing."

There is a particular kind of magic that happens when cherries meet almonds. It is the flavour of a Bakewell tart, of cherry bakewell biscuits, of marzipan and jam — and it turns out to be absolutely wonderful inside a banana loaf. This cherry almond banana bread is tender and buttery, studded with juicy cherries that go soft and jammy in the oven, and finished with a crackly almond-sugar crust that shatters a little when you slice it.

It is the loaf I bake when cherries are in season and the bananas on the counter have gone spotty at exactly the same time. It looks like it came from a bakery — the halved cherries peek out of every slice in deep ruby, and the toasted almonds on top turn golden and crisp — but it is a one-bowl quick bread with about 20 minutes of actual work.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice has cherries in it — not one lonely cherry at the bottom of the loaf, but fruit suspended through the whole crumb, thanks to a simple flour toss. The almond extract gives the loaf a subtle marzipan warmth that makes people ask what the secret ingredient is, and the toasted almonds on top add a crunch that contrasts beautifully with the soft, moist crumb. It uses up three overripe bananas, works with fresh or frozen cherries, and stays moist for days because the fruit keeps feeding moisture back into the crumb.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black skins mean maximum natural sweetness and the softest crumb. Mash them until mostly smooth — a few small lumps are fine and add nice texture. Three medium bananas is the sweet spot; oversized bananas can make the batter too wet to hold the cherries up.

**Cherries.** Fresh sweet cherries, pitted and halved, are ideal in season. Frozen pitted cherries work beautifully year-round — use them straight from the freezer and never thaw them first, or their juice will bleed into the batter. Tart cherries (like Morello) also work, but add 2 extra tablespoons of sugar to balance their sharpness.

**Almond extract.** Just half a teaspoon, but it is the ingredient that makes this loaf taste like a Bakewell tart. It is potent, so measure it — a heavy hand will make the bread taste like marzipan extract rather than almond.

**Toasted sliced almonds.** Toasting is not optional decoration. Raw almonds stay pale and soft in the crumb, while toasted ones turn golden, crisp, and deeply nutty. Two to three minutes in a dry skillet is all it takes — watch them closely, because they go from golden to burnt in seconds.

**Sour cream.** The secret to a tender, bakery-style crumb. Its acidity relaxes the gluten and adds moisture that keeps the loaf soft for days. Full-fat plain Greek yogurt works as a substitute.

**Baking soda + baking powder.** The soda reacts with the acidic bananas and sour cream for lift, while the small amount of baking powder gives extra insurance against the weight of all that fruit. Make sure neither is expired.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The only real technique here is keeping the cherries where they belong — suspended through the loaf instead of sinking to the bottom. The trick is twofold: toss the halved cherries in a tablespoon of flour, and fold them in gently at the very end, after the batter is fully mixed. The flour coating gives the cherries grip, and the thick batter holds them in place as the loaf sets in the oven.

Don't overmix once the flour goes in. Stir just until no dry streaks remain, then fold in the sour cream, then the cherries and almonds. A few lumps are fine; overmixed batter develops gluten and bakes up tough and dense instead of tender.

The toothpick test needs a little judgement here: a cherry will leave a wet red streak on a toothpick even when the bread is done. Test in a spot between the fruit and look for a few moist crumbs, not raw batter. Start checking at 55 minutes, and tent loosely with foil if the almond topping is browning too fast.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. The sugary cherry juices can stick, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Pit cherries fast** with a chopstick: push the blunt end through the stem end and the pit pops out the other side. A sturdy straw works too.
- **Cool before slicing.** 15 minutes in the pan, then fully on a rack. Slicing too early compresses the soft crumb and the warm cherries smear.
- **For clean slices,** use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts — cherry juice stains a blade quickly.
- **Toast the almonds first.** It takes 3 minutes and transforms them from pale and soft to golden and crisp.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Cherry chocolate.** Fold in 1/2 cup of dark chocolate chips with the cherries — cherry and dark chocolate are a classic pairing, and the chocolate goes slightly molten in the warm loaf.

**Amaretto glaze.** Whisk 3/4 cup powdered sugar with 1 tablespoon amaretto (or milk plus 1/4 teaspoon almond extract) and drizzle over the cooled loaf.

**Cherry almond muffins.** Divide the batter among 12 lined muffin cups and bake 22-25 minutes at 350°F — perfect for lunchboxes.

**Whole wheat version.** Swap half the all-purpose flour for whole wheat flour and add an extra tablespoon of sour cream for moisture.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm them for 10 seconds in the microwave so the cherries go soft and jammy again. It is wonderful with salted butter, a smear of cream cheese, or a dollop of clotted cream for dessert. For breakfast, pair with hot coffee or a pot of Earl Grey — the bergamot and almond are made for each other. For a proper dessert, serve a warm slice with a scoop of vanilla ice cream.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. The cherries add moisture, so this loaf keeps especially well — after day 3, move it to the fridge for up to a week. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8-10 minutes or toast them lightly — the edges get deliciously crisp and the almonds re-crisp.
