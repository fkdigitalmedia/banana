instructions:
  - "Heat oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x5-inch loaf pan and line it with parchment paper, leaving an overhang on the long sides for easy lifting."
  - "In a large bowl, whisk the mashed bananas, melted butter, granulated sugar and brown sugar until smooth. Whisk in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla and sour cream."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, baking powder and salt. Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture until just combined — stop as soon as no dry streaks remain. Do not overmix."
  - "In a small bowl, gently toss the raspberries with 1 tablespoon of flour. This light coating helps stop them sinking to the bottom of the loaf."
  - "Fold the floured raspberries and the white chocolate chips into the batter with just a few strokes, taking care not to crush the berries. A little pink streaking is fine — it looks beautiful in the finished loaf."
  - "Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top. Sprinkle over the extra 2 tablespoons of white chocolate chips."
  - "Bake for 55-65 minutes, until the top is golden and a toothpick inserted in the centre comes out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. If the top browns too quickly, tent it loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes."
  - "Cool in the pan for 15 minutes, then lift out using the parchment overhang and cool completely on a wire rack."
  - "For the glaze, whisk the powdered sugar, 1 tablespoon of milk and the vanilla until smooth, adding more milk a teaspoon at a time until it drizzles off a spoon. Drizzle over the cooled loaf, let it set for 15 minutes, then slice with a serrated knife."

There is something quietly luxurious about a slice of banana bread that is not just brown and sweet, but pink-speckled and jewelled. This Raspberry White Chocolate Banana Bread is exactly that: a soft, buttery banana loaf, tart raspberries bursting through the crumb, and creamy white chocolate melting into sweet little pockets. A thin vanilla glaze on top turns it from an everyday bake into something worthy of a brunch table.

It still uses up those spotty bananas on the counter, and it still comes together in one bowl with no mixer. The raspberries do most of the showing off.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice looks as good as it tastes — golden crumb, ruby berries, pale melted chocolate. The tartness of the raspberries cuts through the sweetness of the banana and white chocolate, so the loaf never feels heavy or one-note. It is a genuine all-occasion bake: breakfast with coffee, afternoon tea, a lunchbox treat, or a light dessert with cream. And because the berries are folded in whole, each slice is a little different, which is half the charm.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** The backbone of the loaf. Heavily speckled or black-skinned bananas mash easily and bring natural sweetness and moisture. Underripe bananas make a bland, dry loaf, so wait for the spots.

**Fresh raspberries.** Choose firm, dry berries rather than soft or damp ones, and handle them as little as possible. Tossing them in a tablespoon of flour is a small step that pays off — it helps suspend them in the batter instead of letting them all sink.

**White chocolate chips.** White chocolate is sweet and creamy rather than cocoa-rich, which is exactly why it works here: it softens the raspberries' sharpness without competing with the banana. Use real white chocolate chips (with cocoa butter on the ingredients list) if you can, as cheaper candy melts stay waxy.

**Sour cream or Greek yogurt.** This is what keeps the crumb moist for days, even with juicy berries in the mix. Its gentle acidity also helps the baking soda lift the loaf properly.

**Two sugars.** Granulated sugar keeps the crumb light and golden, while a little brown sugar adds a hint of caramel depth. Together they balance the tart berries better than either alone.

**Baking soda plus baking powder.** The soda reacts with the acidic bananas and sour cream, while the small amount of baking powder gives extra lift to support the weight of the berries and chocolate.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The method is classic quick-bread, with one golden rule: fold, do not stir, once the berries go in. Overmixing after the flour is added develops gluten and makes a tough loaf; crushing the raspberries turns the batter an even, muddy pink instead of leaving distinct ruby pockets. So mix the batter until just combined, toss the berries in flour, then fold them in with three or four gentle strokes and stop.

Pour the batter into the lined pan and sprinkle the extra white chocolate chips on top — they melt into golden spots on the crust. The loaf needs a full 55 to 65 minutes because the berries add moisture. Trust the toothpick test over the clock: it should come out with moist crumbs, not wet batter. If the top is darkening faster than the centre is baking, a loose foil tent for the last 15 minutes solves it without drying the loaf out.

Cooling matters more than usual here. Fifteen minutes in the pan lets the crumb set, then lift the loaf out and cool it completely before glazing or slicing. Cutting too early will squash the soft berry pockets and smear the glaze.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang. Berry juices can stick, and the sling lifts the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Room temperature eggs** blend into the melted butter smoothly instead of seizing it. Five minutes in warm water is enough if you forgot to take them out.
- **Do not skip the flour toss** for the raspberries — it really does reduce sinking, especially with larger berries.
- **For clean slices,** use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts, so the berries do not drag through the crumb.
- **Glaze only a fully cooled loaf.** Warm bread melts the glaze into a thin, patchy coating instead of a pretty drizzle.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Lemon raspberry.** Add 1 tablespoon of finely grated lemon zest to the wet ingredients and replace the milk in the glaze with lemon juice. The citrus lifts the whole loaf.

**Dark chocolate raspberry.** Swap the white chocolate chips for dark chocolate chips for a sharper, less sweet version — a favourite for dessert, served slightly warm.

**Almond crunch.** Fold in 1/2 cup of sliced almonds with the berries and sprinkle more on top before baking for a toasty crunch.

**Mixed berry.** Replace half the raspberries with blueberries for a mixed-berry loaf. Keep the total berry weight the same so the crumb stays balanced.

**Muffin version.** Bake as 12 muffins at 350°F for 20-24 minutes — ideal for lunchboxes and freezing in portions.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm a slice for 10 seconds in the microwave so the white chocolate just starts to melt again. It is lovely plain, but a dollop of clotted cream, mascarpone or lightly whipped cream turns it into dessert. For breakfast or brunch, pair it with coffee or a pot of Earl Grey — the bergamot notes suit raspberries beautifully. In summer, serve with a handful of extra fresh raspberries and a drizzle of cream.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Because of the fresh berries, this loaf keeps a little less long than plain banana bread: airtight at room temperature for up to 2 days, then refrigerated for up to 5 days. Bring refrigerated slices back to room temperature, or warm them gently, before serving. To freeze, skip the glaze, wrap individual slices tightly in plastic wrap and then foil, and freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature, then glaze fresh if you like. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8-10 minutes — the edges crisp up nicely while the berries stay soft.
