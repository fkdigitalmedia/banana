Some banana breads are polite. This one is not. It's the campfire classic folded into a loaf — gooey marshmallow pockets, pools of melted milk chocolate, and crunchy graham cracker in every slice, with a toasted marshmallow topping that goes golden and sticky in the oven. It tastes like the last night of summer, and it uses up three spotty bananas while it's at it.

The trick is balance. The banana bread itself is the familiar moist, buttery base — sour cream keeps it tender, brown sugar gives it caramel depth — and the s'mores elements are folded in at just the right ratio so the loaf still holds together and slices cleanly.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice has all three s'mores layers — chocolate, marshmallow, and graham — instead of a plain banana bread with a token sprinkle on top. The marshmallows melt into gooey pockets as it bakes, the graham crumbs in the batter add a honeyed crunch, and the toasted marshmallow topping looks spectacular with zero extra effort. It's a kid-favourite that adults fight over, it comes together in one bowl, and it turns a regular banana bread into the bake everyone asks about.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black skins mean maximum sweetness and moisture. Mash until mostly smooth — a few small lumps are fine. If your bananas aren't ripe enough, bake them unpeeled at 300°F for 15–20 minutes until the skins blacken.

**Mini marshmallows.** Mini marshmallows distribute evenly through the batter; large ones create big gooey gaps. Keep 1/2 cup aside for the topping — those are the ones that toast golden on the surface.

**Milk chocolate chips (or chopped chocolate).** Milk chocolate is the classic s'mores flavour, but chopped chocolate bars melt into bigger, more dramatic pools than chips. Either works — use what you have.

**Graham cracker crumbs and pieces.** Crumbs go into the dry ingredients for flavour in every bite; broken pieces fold in for crunch. Crush the sheets in a zip-top bag with a rolling pin — you want crumbs plus some small chunks, not dust.

**Sour cream.** The secret to a tender crumb that stays moist for days. Its acidity relaxes the gluten and balances the sweetness of the marshmallow. Full-fat plain Greek yogurt works as a substitute.

**Cinnamon.** Just a teaspoon, in the background — it warms up the chocolate and graham flavours without turning the loaf into a spice bread.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

Start with the graham crackers: crush about 5 full sheets into crumbs and break another couple of sheets into small chunks. Having both textures is what makes this loaf work — the crumbs flavour the crumb, the chunks give you something to bite into.

Mix the wet ingredients in one bowl and the dry in another, then fold them together just until no dry streaks remain. Overmixing develops gluten and bakes up tough, which is the last thing you want under all that gooey topping. Fold the chocolate, marshmallows, and graham pieces in with a spatula, not a whisk — you want them evenly scattered, not crushed.

The topping marshmallows go on last, right before the pan goes in the oven. They'll puff, soften, and toast to a pale gold. If they start browning too fast — usually in the last 15 minutes — tent the loaf loosely with foil and let the centre finish baking.

Start checking at 55 minutes. The toothpick test is tricky here because melted chocolate looks wet — look for moist crumbs clinging to the toothpick rather than raw batter. And let the loaf cool properly: the marshmallow topping needs time to set, and slicing too early compresses the soft crumb.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. Melted marshmallow and chocolate stick, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Room temperature egg** blends smoothly instead of seizing the melted butter. Quick fix: put the egg in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Wipe the knife between slices.** A serrated bread knife and a quick wipe keeps the marshmallow from dragging and gives clean, photogenic slices.
- **Don't add extra marshmallows to the batter.** It sounds generous, but too many melt into large pockets that make the loaf sink. The written amounts are the sweet spot.
- **For the campfire finish,** pass a kitchen torch over the topping after cooling, or broil slices for 30 seconds — watch constantly, it goes from golden to burnt in seconds.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Dark chocolate s'mores.** Swap the milk chocolate for 55–70% dark chocolate and add a pinch of flaky sea salt on top — the bitterness balances the marshmallow beautifully.

**Peanut butter s'mores.** Swirl 1/3 cup of creamy peanut butter through the batter before baking, and swap half the chocolate chips for peanut butter chips.

**Nutty crunch.** Fold in 1/2 cup of chopped toasted pecans or walnuts with the graham pieces for extra texture.

**Fluff swirl.** Instead of topping marshmallows, dollop 1/2 cup of marshmallow fluff over the batter and swirl it in with a knife — it bakes into a rippled, sticky layer.

**Chocolate graham.** Use chocolate graham crackers instead of plain for a double-chocolate version.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm them for 10 seconds in the microwave so the chocolate re-melts. It's wonderful with a cold glass of milk, a scoop of vanilla ice cream, or a drizzle of warm chocolate sauce for dessert. For a campfire-at-home moment, toast a slice under the broiler and serve it with hot chocolate. Kids love it as an after-school treat; adults love it with coffee.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days — the marshmallow topping stays soft and gooey. For longer storage, wrap individual slices in plastic wrap and freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes, or give them a 10-second microwave zap to re-melt the chocolate. Don't refrigerate the whole loaf — the marshmallow topping firms up and turns rubbery.
