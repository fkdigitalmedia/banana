instructions:
  - "Heat oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x5-inch loaf pan and line with parchment, leaving an overhang on the long sides for easy lifting."
  - "In a large bowl, whisk the mashed bananas, melted butter, brown sugar, granulated sugar, eggs, vanilla, and sour cream until smooth and well combined."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, salt, and cardamom. Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture until just combined — a few small lumps are fine. Don't overmix."
  - "Toss the chopped pistachios and white chocolate chips with 1 tablespoon of the flour mixture in a small bowl (this keeps them from sinking), then fold them gently into the batter."
  - "Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top. Sprinkle evenly with the finely chopped pistachios and coarse sugar."
  - "Bake 55–65 minutes, until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs (melted white chocolate doesn't count). If the top browns too quickly, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes."
  - "Cool 15 minutes in the pan, then lift out using the parchment and cool completely on a wire rack before slicing. The white chocolate firms up as it cools, so the slices hold together best once fully cool."

Pistachios and banana bread were made for each other. The loaf itself is soft, buttery, and deeply banana-flavoured, but every bite has a little crunch from the chopped pistachios and a creamy, melt-in-your-mouth pocket of white chocolate. A pinch of cardamom ties the whole thing together with a warm, floral note that makes the pistachios taste even more like themselves.

It looks like a bakery loaf, too. The pistachio-and-sugar topping bakes into a crackly, jewel-green crust that makes slicing it feel like a small occasion — which is handy, because this is the loaf you bake when you want people to ask for the recipe.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

The contrast is everything here: soft, moist crumb against crunchy pistachios, sweet banana against salty nuts, creamy white chocolate against a crackly topping. It uses up three spotty bananas, comes together in one bowl plus one for the dry ingredients, and needs no mixer. The pistachios turn a familiar banana bread into something that tastes genuinely special — the kind of loaf that disappears before it has fully cooled.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black skins give maximum sweetness and moisture. Mash them until mostly smooth; a few small lumps are fine and add texture. If your bananas are still yellow, ripen them fast by baking unpeeled at 300°F for 15–20 minutes until the skins blacken.

**Shelled pistachios.** Buy them already shelled — shelling a cup's worth by hand is nobody's idea of fun. Roughly chop them so you get a mix of chunky pieces and small bits; the small bits flavour the crumb, the big ones give the crunch. Salted pistachios are ideal here because the salt tames the white chocolate's sweetness.

**White chocolate.** Use chips or chop a bar into rough 1/2-inch pieces — the bar gives you those lovely irregular melted pockets. White chocolate is sweet, so it works best against the salty pistachios and the not-too-sweet banana batter.

**Cardamom.** Just half a teaspoon, but it makes a real difference — cardamom and pistachio are a classic pairing for a reason. Freshly ground cardamom is best; pre-ground loses its fragrance faster than most spices, so check yours still smells bright and floral.

**Sour cream.** Keeps the crumb tender and moist for days, and its acidity helps the baking soda do its lifting. Full-fat plain Greek yogurt is a fine substitute.

**Brown sugar + a little white sugar.** Brown sugar brings caramel warmth; the white sugar helps the topping crisp up and keeps the crumb from getting too dense.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The only real technique here is keeping the mix-ins suspended in the batter. Toss the pistachios and white chocolate in a spoonful of flour before folding them in — the flour coating grips the batter and stops them sinking to the bottom of the pan during the long bake. Fold gently and stop as soon as they're distributed; overmixing after the flour goes in makes a tough loaf.

Spread the topping evenly before baking — finely chopped pistachios and coarse sugar — and press it very lightly into the batter so it stays put. It bakes into a crackly, golden-green crust that looks as good as it tastes.

Start checking at 55 minutes. The toothpick test needs a little judgement here because melted white chocolate looks wet — look for moist crumbs rather than raw batter clinging to the toothpick. If the top is browning too fast (the pistachios can catch), tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes.

Let the loaf cool completely before slicing. This matters more than usual: the white chocolate is molten when hot and needs time to firm up, or your slices will fall apart and smear. Fifteen minutes in the pan, then fully cool on a rack.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Chop the pistachios on a board, not in a food processor.** A processor turns them to dust in seconds; a knife gives you the chunky-smooth mix this loaf needs.
- **Room temperature eggs** blend into the batter smoothly instead of seizing the melted butter. Quick fix: put the eggs in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. The topping can stick, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **For clean slices,** use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts — the white chocolate can drag.
- **Toast the pistachios first** (5 minutes in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking often) for a deeper, nuttier flavour. Let them cool before chopping.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Rosewater and pistachio.** Add 1/2 teaspoon of rosewater with the vanilla for a Middle Eastern twist — rose, pistachio, and cardamom are a classic trio.

**Dark chocolate instead of white.** Swap the white chocolate for dark chocolate chunks for a less sweet, more grown-up loaf. The pistachios still shine, and the contrast is gorgeous.

**Orange zest.** Add 1 tablespoon of finely grated orange zest to the wet ingredients — citrus lifts the pistachio and plays beautifully with the banana.

**Pistachio cream swirl.** Warm 1/3 cup of pistachio cream (the spreadable kind) until pourable and drizzle it between layers of batter, then drag a knife through once — a green, nutty ribbon through every slice.

**Muffin version.** Divide the batter between 12 lined muffin cups and bake at 350°F for 20–24 minutes. Sprinkle the topping on each one before baking.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, when the white chocolate has set but the crumb is still soft. It's wonderful with a smear of salted butter, or alongside a cup of masala chai — the cardamom in the loaf echoes the spices in the tea. For dessert, warm a slice for 10 seconds and serve with vanilla ice cream and a drizzle of honey. A few extra chopped pistachios scattered over the plate makes it look restaurant-ready.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days, or refrigerate for up to a week — the white chocolate firms in the fridge, so bring slices back to room temperature before serving. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes to re-crisp the topping, or toast them lightly — the edges get deliciously crisp and the white chocolate softens just enough.
