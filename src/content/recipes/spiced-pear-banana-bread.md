  - "In a large bowl, whisk the mashed bananas, melted butter, brown sugar, granulated sugar, eggs, vanilla, and Greek yogurt until smooth and well combined."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, and cloves."
  - "Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture until just combined — a few small lumps are fine. Do not overmix."

There is a short window in autumn when pears are at their best and bananas on the counter are going spotty at exactly the same time — and this loaf is what that happy coincidence is for. Ripe bananas bring the sweetness and the familiar banana-bread softness, while diced pear bakes into little jammy pockets that stay tender and fragrant. Around them, cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg and a whisper of clove turn the whole thing into the loaf you want with a mug of tea on a cold afternoon.

It is a one-bowl-wet, one-bowl-dry bake, no mixer required, and the only real skill is choosing a pear that is ripe but still firm enough to dice. Everything else is just stirring and waiting for the house to smell wonderful.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice has golden pieces of pear tucked through a warmly spiced crumb — it looks and tastes like autumn without being a novelty bake. The cinnamon-sugar topping bakes into a lightly crunchy, sparkly crust, the Greek yogurt keeps the loaf moist for days, and the spice blend is balanced so no single spice shouts over the fruit. It uses up three overripe bananas and one pear, and it is just as good on day three as it is warm from the oven.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black skins mean maximum sweetness and the deepest banana flavour. Mash until mostly smooth; a few small lumps are fine.

**Ripe but firm pear.** Bartlett or Bosc are ideal. Dice it small — about 1/4-inch pieces — so it distributes evenly and bakes into soft pockets rather than wet chunks. Peel first; pear skin stays tough in a quick bread.

**Warm spice blend.** Cinnamon leads, ginger adds brightness, nutmeg adds depth, and a small amount of clove gives that unmistakable autumn warmth. Measure the clove carefully — more than 1/4 teaspoon will dominate.

**Brown sugar plus a little white sugar.** Brown sugar brings molasses warmth and moisture; the white sugar helps the topping crisp and keeps the crumb from getting too dense.

**Greek yogurt or sour cream.** Either works. The acidity relaxes the gluten for a tender crumb and adds moisture that keeps the loaf soft for days.

**Melted butter.** Melting rather than creaming gives a denser, moister quick-bread crumb — exactly right here, since the pear already adds tenderness.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

Start by dicing the pear before you mix anything, so the batter is not sitting around while you chop. Keep the pieces small and even; uneven dice means some pieces stay firm while others dissolve.

Fold the dry ingredients into the wet only until no dry streaks remain. Overmixing develops gluten and bakes up tough, and with fruit in the batter that toughness is even more noticeable. Fold the pear in last, gently, so the pieces stay intact.

Smooth the batter into the pan and sprinkle the cinnamon sugar evenly right up to the edges — the edges are where the crust gets its best crunch. If you are using walnuts, scatter them over the sugar so they toast in the oven rather than sinking.

Start checking at 60 minutes. The toothpick test needs a little judgement here: a toothpick that hits a pear piece will come out wet even when the loaf is done. Test in two or three spots and look for moist crumbs, not raw batter. Tent with foil if the top is browning faster than the centre is setting.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Toss juicy pear dice in flour.** If your pear is very ripe and juicy, toss the dice in 1 tablespoon of the measured flour before folding in. It absorbs surface moisture and keeps the crumb even.
- **Line the pan with parchment.** The cinnamon-sugar top can stick, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Room temperature eggs** blend smoothly into the batter instead of seizing the melted butter. Quick fix: put the eggs in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Cool completely before slicing.** The pear pieces are soft and the crumb is tender when warm; a full cool on a rack gives clean slices and lets the spice flavour settle.
- **Use a serrated knife** and wipe it between cuts for the neatest slices.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Pear and walnut loaf.** Fold 1/2 cup of chopped toasted walnuts into the batter along with the pear for crunch in every bite.

**Gingerbread spice.** Swap the spice blend for 2 teaspoons of gingerbread spice mix and add 2 tablespoons of molasses to the wet ingredients for a darker, festive loaf.

**Streusel top.** Replace the cinnamon sugar with a quick streusel — 1/3 cup flour, 1/4 cup brown sugar, 1/4 cup cold butter rubbed together — for a crumbly, bakery-style crust.

**Chocolate chip pear bread.** Fold in 1/2 cup of dark chocolate chips with the pear. Chocolate and pear are a classic pairing, and the chips melt into little pools.

**Maple glaze.** Once cooled, drizzle with a glaze of 1/2 cup powdered sugar whisked with 2 tablespoons of maple syrup for a sweet, autumnal finish.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm them for 10 seconds in the microwave so the pear pockets go soft again. It is wonderful with salted butter, a smear of cream cheese, or a dollop of softly whipped cream for dessert. For breakfast, pair it with hot coffee or chai — the ginger and cinnamon in the loaf echo the spices in the tea beautifully. A slice toasted lightly in a dry pan, edges crisped, is hard to beat.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Because of the fresh fruit, move it to the fridge after day 3, where it keeps up to a week — bring slices back to room temperature before serving. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes or toast them lightly — the cinnamon-sugar crust crisps up again beautifully.
