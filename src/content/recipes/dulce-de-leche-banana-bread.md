Bananas and caramel were always going to be friends — and dulce de leche is the caramel that takes the friendship seriously. This banana bread is buttery, golden and impossibly moist, marbled with thick ribbons of milky caramel that stay soft and gooey even when the loaf is completely cool. Toasted pecans add crunch against the tender crumb, and a drizzle of warm dulce de leche over the top soaks into the crackly sugar crust in glossy ribbons. It is the loaf to bake when plain banana bread feels too plain — rich enough for dessert, but still unmistakably banana bread.

This is the one to make for a weekend bake that disappears before it is fully cool, for a holiday table, or for a cake tin at work that earns you a reputation. It looks beautiful sliced — golden crumb, pale pecan flecks, glossy caramel ribbons — and it keeps beautifully for days.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

The dulce de leche swirl is the star — it marbles through the crumb in glossy, milky-caramel ribbons so every slice has pockets of soft, gooey caramel. Unlike plain caramel sauce, dulce de leche is thick enough to hold its shape in the batter, so the swirl survives baking instead of dissolving into sweetness. Toasting the pecans first makes them crunchy and buttery instead of soft, and the turbinado sugar bakes into a crackly top that shatters gently under the knife. It is a one-bowl, 20-minute-hands-on bake that eats like something from a proper patisserie, and it is endlessly adaptable: swap in walnuts, add a spoonful of espresso powder, or serve it warm with ice cream.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black-skinned bananas are essential — they bring the sweetness and moisture this loaf needs. Mash until mostly smooth; a few small lumps add nice texture. Measure the mash: 1 1/4 cups is the target. Too much banana makes a thin batter, and a thin batter cannot hold a heavy caramel swirl in place — it will sink to the bottom.

**Dulce de leche.** This is the ingredient that makes the loaf. Buy it in a jar or can from the baking aisle or the Latin foods section, or make it by simmering a can of sweetened condensed milk (label removed) in a pot of water for 2-3 hours, keeping the can covered with water the whole time. Whatever you do, warm it only until it pours in a thick ribbon — if it runs like cream, it is too thin and will disappear into the batter.

**Brown sugar.** Light brown sugar does double duty here: it sweetens the crumb and deepens the caramel flavour, echoing the dulce de leche instead of competing with it. Pack it firmly into the measuring cup — a loose scoop can be a quarter-cup short.

**Toasted pecans.** This is the crunch that keeps the loaf from being one-note soft. Raw pecans are soft and a little waxy; seven minutes in the oven makes them buttery, fragrant and crisp. Buy them fresh and taste one before baking; stale nuts will ruin an otherwise perfect loaf.

**Cinnamon and nutmeg.** Just enough spice to warm the loaf without turning it into a spice cake. The cinnamon leans into the caramel, and the nutmeg adds a quiet, buttery depth in the background. Measure the nutmeg carefully — a heavy hand makes the loaf taste medicinal.

**Turbinado sugar.** The coarse crystals on top bake into a crackly, golden crust that shatters gently under the knife. It is the first thing you notice about a slice, and it contrasts beautifully with the soft, caramel-swirled crumb underneath.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The only technique that matters here is the toasting and the marbling. Start by toasting the pecans until fragrant and a shade darker, then chop them roughly while they cool. Warm the dulce de leche gently until it loosens into a thick, pourable ribbon — this is the step that decides whether you get ribbons or a puddle. While the pecans cool, whisk the wet ingredients smooth in one bowl and the dry ingredients together in another.

Fold the dry into the wet until just combined — a few streaks of flour are fine; over-stirring develops gluten and bakes up tough instead of tender. Then fold in the pecans. Pour half the batter into the lined pan, dollop half the dulce de leche over it, add the remaining batter, then the rest of the dulce de leche on top. Now marble: run a butter knife through the batter in a loose figure-eight, two or three passes only. That is the whole trick — gentle passes leave ribbons, enthusiastic stirring mixes the caramel away. Finish with the reserved pecans and turbinado sugar scattered evenly over the surface.

Start checking at 55 minutes: a toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. A smear of dulce de leche on the toothpick does not count as underbaked — look for batter. The top should be deep golden and crackly — if it is browning before the centre is done, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes.

Cooling matters, and so does the drizzle. While the loaf is still warm from the pan, drizzle the reserved dulce de leche over the top so it soaks into the crust in glossy ribbons. Then let the loaf cool a full 45 minutes on the rack before slicing, or the soft, caramel-swirled crumb will compress and the warm swirl will smear. Use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts for clean slices that show off the marbling.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Warm the dulce de leche — but only just.** Fifteen seconds in the microwave until it pours in a thick ribbon. If it runs like cream, let it cool a few minutes. Too thin and it vanishes into the batter; too cold and it sits in a clump instead of marbling.
- **Measure the mashed banana.** Three bananas of any size is not a measurement — 1 1/4 cups is. Too much banana makes a thin batter, and a thin batter lets the swirl sink to the bottom.
- **Drizzle while warm.** The reserved dulce de leche goes on while the loaf is still warm from the pan, so it soaks into the crust instead of sitting on top. A cold loaf gets a sticky coating; a warm loaf gets glossy ribbons that sink in.
- **Marble gently.** Two or three figure-eight passes with a butter knife are plenty. More than that and the dulce de leche disappears into the batter instead of leaving ribbons.
- **Toast the pecans until they smell like a bakery.** Seven to eight minutes at 350°F, until fragrant and a shade darker. Taste one before baking — stale nuts will ruin the loaf.
- **Don't slice warm.** The caramel-swirled crumb needs time to set or it compresses under the knife. Forty-five minutes on the rack is the hardest part of this recipe — and the most important.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Espresso dulce de leche.** Add 1 tablespoon of instant espresso powder to the dry ingredients. You will not taste coffee — it just makes the caramel taste deeper and more complex. This is the version to bake for serious caramel lovers.

**Chocolate chip dulce de leche.** Fold in 3/4 cup of dark chocolate chunks with the pecans. The chocolate melts into fudgy pools that pair beautifully with the caramel — a little like a chocolate-covered toffee, in banana bread form.

**Salted dulce de leche.** Sprinkle 1/2 teaspoon of flaky sea salt over the drizzle while the loaf is still warm. The salt sharpens the caramel and keeps the sweetness in check — this is the version that disappears fastest.

**Walnut dulce de leche.** Swap the pecans for toasted walnuts. They are a little more bitter and a little more rugged, which stands up beautifully to the sweet caramel.

**Dulce de leche muffins.** Divide the batter between 12 lined muffin cups, spooning a little warmed dulce de leche into each as you fill them, and top each with chopped pecans and a pinch of turbinado sugar. Bake at 350°F for 20-23 minutes. They are perfect for lunchboxes and bake sales.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm a slice for 10 seconds in the microwave so the caramel goes soft and gooey again and the crumb turns pillowy. It is wonderful with a smear of salted butter, a spoonful of extra dulce de leche, or a dollop of mascarpone, and it makes a superb base for a scoop of vanilla or coffee ice cream. For the full café experience, pair it with a flat white or a mug of black coffee — the bitterness plays beautifully against the rich, milky sweetness. For dessert, a warm slice with vanilla ice cream and a drizzle of warm dulce de leche is hard to beat.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days — the sugar top stays crackly for the first day and softens slightly after that, while the crumb stays moist and the caramel ribbons stay soft. After day 3, refrigerate for up to a week and bring slices back to room temperature before eating. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8-10 minutes or toast them lightly in a dry pan — the edges get deliciously crisp, the caramel goes soft and gooey again, and the whole kitchen smells like a caramel shop.
