  - "In a large bowl, whisk the mashed bananas, melted butter, sugar, eggs, vanilla, and sour cream until smooth and well combined."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk together the flour, sifted matcha powder, baking soda, and salt. Sifting the matcha is essential — unsifted matcha clumps and leaves bitter green specks in the crumb."

Most banana bread is brown and familiar — and then there is this one, sliced open to reveal a soft, pale-green crumb with pools of melted white chocolate. Matcha banana bread sounds unusual until the first bite: the earthy, gently grassy green tea settles against the caramel sweetness of very ripe bananas, and the white chocolate chips melt into creamy pockets that tie the whole thing together. It tastes like something from a Kyoto café, but it bakes in one bowl with bananas you were about to throw away.

It is also a beautiful loaf to serve. The green crumb photographs wonderfully, the white chocolate on top goes golden and glossy in the oven, and nobody at the table has had banana bread quite like it before.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice is a conversation starter — that vivid green crumb is genuinely striking, and the flavor backs it up. The matcha brings a subtle, sophisticated earthiness that makes the banana taste even sweeter by contrast, without any bitterness. It uses three spotty bananas, comes together without a mixer in about 15 minutes of hands-on work, and the white chocolate chips mean every bite has a creamy surprise. It is also endlessly adaptable: swap the chips for nuts, dust it with matcha sugar, or serve it warm with green tea ice cream.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black-skinned bananas are essential — they bring the sweetness and moisture that balance the matcha's earthy edge. Mash until mostly smooth; a few small lumps add nice texture. If your bananas aren't ripe enough, bake them unpeeled at 300°F for 15–20 minutes until the skins blacken.

**Culinary-grade matcha powder.** This is the ingredient that makes or breaks the loaf. Culinary grade is milled for cooking, has a bold flavor that survives baking, and costs much less than ceremonial grade. Sift it through a fine-mesh strainer before whisking it into the flour — matcha clumps stubbornly, and unsifted powder leaves bitter green specks. Use fresh matcha with a vivid, bright-green color; dull, yellowish matcha is stale and will taste flat.

**White chocolate chips.** They do two jobs here: sweetness and contrast. Their creaminess softens matcha's grassy notes, and the chips on top go glossy and golden as they bake. Use real white chocolate chips (cocoa butter on the ingredients list), not white baking chips, which are mostly palm oil and don't melt the same way.

**Sour cream.** The secret to a tender, bakery-style crumb. Its acidity relaxes the gluten and adds moisture that keeps the loaf soft for days. Full-fat plain Greek yogurt works as a substitute.

**Melted butter.** Melting rather than creaming gives a denser, moister crumb — exactly what you want in a quick bread. Let it cool slightly before whisking in the eggs so they don't scramble.

**Baking soda.** The only leavener here. It reacts with the acidic bananas and sour cream for lift, so make sure yours isn't expired — drop a pinch in vinegar; it should fizz vigorously.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The only real technique in this recipe is the matcha. Sift it. I mean it — whisking matcha straight into the flour looks fine until you cut the loaf and find dark green lumps. A fine-mesh strainer and 30 seconds of tapping solves it completely. Once sifted, whisk it thoroughly with the flour, baking soda, and salt so the color is even before it meets the wet ingredients.

From there it is classic banana bread: wet ingredients whisked smooth in one bowl, dry folded in until just combined. Fold the white chocolate chips in gently at the end — over-stirring at this stage develops gluten and toughens the crumb. A few lumps in the batter are fine; they bake out.

Pour the batter into the lined pan, smooth the top, and scatter the reserved chips over the surface. They sink slightly as the loaf rises and bake into golden, glossy spots. Start checking at 55 minutes: a toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not raw batter. The top should be set with lightly golden edges — the green crumb browns faster than a plain loaf looks like it should, so trust the toothpick, not the color.

Cooling matters more than usual here. The loaf needs a full 45 minutes on the rack before slicing or the soft green crumb will compress and the white chocolate will smear. Use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts for clean slices.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Sift the matcha twice if it is at all lumpy.** Clumps are the single most common flaw in matcha baking, and they taste bitter.
- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. The melted white chocolate can stick, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Room temperature eggs** blend into the batter smoothly instead of seizing the melted butter. Quick fix: put the eggs in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Tent with foil** if the top is browning before the center is done — matcha's green color fades with too much direct heat, so protecting the top keeps the crumb greener.
- **For the greenest crumb,** use fresh matcha and don't overbake. Even 5 extra minutes in the oven dulls the color noticeably.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Matcha macadamia.** Swap the white chocolate chips for 3/4 cup chopped toasted macadamia nuts — buttery, crunchy, and a perfect match for green tea.

**Dark chocolate matcha.** Use dark chocolate chips instead of white for a more grown-up, bittersweet loaf. The dark chocolate and matcha together taste almost like a Japanese chocolate bar.

**Matcha sugar dusting.** After cooling, dust the top with a mix of 2 tablespoons powdered sugar and 1 teaspoon matcha sifted together. It looks stunning and adds a little extra green-tea hit.

**Coconut matcha.** Fold in 1/2 cup of sweetened shredded coconut with the chips — tropical, chewy, and lovely with the banana.

**Matcha cream cheese swirl.** Beat 4 oz softened cream cheese with 2 tablespoons sugar and 1 egg yolk, and dollop it between two layers of batter. The tangy swirl against the green crumb is gorgeous sliced.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm a slice for 10 seconds in the microwave to re-melt the white chocolate. It is wonderful with a smear of salted butter, a spoonful of mascarpone, or a drizzle of honey. For the full café experience, pair it with a whisked matcha latte or a pot of green tea — the tea echoes the loaf's flavor and makes the banana taste sweeter. For dessert, a slice alongside vanilla or green tea ice cream is hard to beat.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. The sour cream keeps it moist, but after day 3 move it to the fridge for up to a week — matcha loaves keep their flavor well chilled. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes or toast them lightly — the edges get deliciously crisp and the white chocolate goes melty again.
