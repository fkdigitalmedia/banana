Hazelnuts and chocolate were made for each other, and bananas are the perfect excuse to bring them together in a loaf pan. This banana bread is dark, fudgy and deeply chocolatey, studded with crunchy toasted hazelnuts and pockets of melted dark chocolate, with a ribbon of warm chocolate-hazelnut spread marbled through the crumb. A sprinkle of turbinado sugar and extra chopped hazelnuts bakes into a crackly, sparkly top that shatters gently under the knife. It is the loaf to bake when plain banana bread feels too plain — rich enough for dessert, but still unmistakably banana bread.

This is the one to make for chocolate lovers, for a weekend bake that disappears before it is fully cool, or for a cake tin at work that earns you a reputation. It looks beautiful sliced — dark crumb, pale hazelnut flecks, glossy ribbons of chocolate — and it keeps beautifully for days.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

The hazelnut swirl is the star — it marbles through the crumb in glossy, praline-like ribbons so every slice has pockets of creamy chocolate-hazelnut flavour. Toasting the hazelnuts first makes them crunchy and sweet instead of soft and bitter, and the dark chocolate chunks melt into fudgy pools as the loaf bakes. It is a one-bowl, 20-minute-hands-on bake that eats like something from a proper patisserie, and it is endlessly adaptable: swap in white chocolate, add a spoonful of espresso powder, or serve it warm with ice cream.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black-skinned bananas are essential — they bring the sweetness and moisture this loaf needs. Mash until mostly smooth; a few small lumps add nice texture. Measure the mash: 1 1/4 cups is the target. Too much banana makes a thin batter, and a thin batter lets the chocolate chunks sink to the bottom.

**Dutch-process cocoa powder.** Dutch-process cocoa is less acidic than natural cocoa, so it bakes up darker, smoother and more intensely chocolatey — exactly what this loaf wants. Natural cocoa works too, but the crumb will be lighter and slightly more acidic. Either way, sift it: cocoa powder clumps badly, and an unsifted lump in the crumb is a bitter surprise.

**Toasted hazelnuts.** This is the ingredient that makes or breaks the loaf. Raw hazelnuts are soft, a little bitter and wrapped in papery skins. Ten minutes in the oven transforms them — crunchy, sweet, nutty, and the skins rub right off in a kitchen towel. Buy them fresh and taste one before baking; stale nuts will ruin an otherwise perfect loaf.

**Dark chocolate chunks.** Chopped dark chocolate (60-70%) melts into fudgy pools that chips never quite manage, and the irregular pieces give you big melty pockets and small flecks. Toss them in a tablespoon of the flour mixture before folding in — the light coating helps them grip the batter instead of sinking.

**The chocolate-hazelnut swirl.** A third of a cup of chocolate-hazelnut spread, warmed until it pours in a thick ribbon, marbles through the batter the same way a cinnamon swirl does. Warm it gently — 10 seconds in the microwave — or it will sit in a clump instead of marbling.

**Turbinado sugar.** The coarse crystals on top bake into a crackly, sparkly crust that shatters gently under the knife. It is the first thing you notice about a slice, and it contrasts beautifully with the fudgy crumb underneath.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The only technique that matters here is the toasting and the marbling. Start by toasting the hazelnuts until fragrant and the skins split, then rub them in a towel to remove the skins and chop them roughly. While they cool, whisk the wet ingredients smooth in one bowl and sift the dry ingredients together in another — sifting the cocoa is not optional.

Fold the dry into the wet until just combined — a few streaks of flour are fine; over-stirring develops gluten and bakes up tough instead of tender. Then fold in the hazelnuts and chocolate chunks. Pour half the batter into the lined pan, dollop half the warmed hazelnut spread over it, add the remaining batter, then the rest of the spread on top. Now marble: run a butter knife through the batter in a loose figure-eight, two or three passes only. That is the whole trick — gentle passes leave ribbons, enthusiastic stirring mixes the spread away. Finish with the reserved hazelnuts, chocolate chunks and turbinado sugar scattered evenly over the surface.

Start checking at 60 minutes: a toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. Melted chocolate on the toothpick does not count as underbaked — look for batter. The top should be dark and crackly — if it is browning before the centre is done, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes.

Cooling matters. The loaf needs a full 45 minutes on the rack before slicing, or the soft, fudgy crumb will compress and the warm swirl will smear. Use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts for clean slices that show off the marbling.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Toast the hazelnuts until they smell like a bakery.** Ten to twelve minutes at 350°F, until fragrant and the skins are splitting. Rub the hot nuts in a clean kitchen towel and most of the skins come right off — a few stubborn bits are fine.
- **Measure the mashed banana.** Three bananas of any size is not a measurement — 1 1/4 cups is. Too much banana makes a thin batter, and a thin batter lets the chocolate chunks sink.
- **Toss the chocolate in flour.** A tablespoon of the flour mixture tossed with the chunks before folding in helps them grip the batter. Chopped chocolate bars sink less than smooth, heavy chips.
- **Warm the hazelnut spread.** Ten seconds in the microwave until it pours in a thick ribbon. Cold spread from the jar will not marble — it just sits in a clump.
- **Marble gently.** Two or three figure-eight passes with a butter knife are plenty. More than that and the spread disappears into the batter instead of leaving ribbons.
- **Don't slice warm.** The fudgy crumb needs time to set or it compresses under the knife. Forty-five minutes on the rack is the hardest part of this recipe — and the most important.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Espresso hazelnut.** Add 1 tablespoon of instant espresso powder to the dry ingredients. You will not taste coffee — it just makes the chocolate taste more chocolatey. This is the version to bake for serious chocolate lovers.

**White chocolate hazelnut.** Replace the dark chocolate chunks with white chocolate chunks and skip the cinnamon. The loaf bakes up sweeter and creamier, with pale, melty pockets against the dark crumb — beautiful sliced.

**Hazelnut praline crunch.** Fold in 1/2 cup of crushed hazelnut praline or chopped toffee with the dry ingredients. It melts into caramel pockets and adds a buttery crunch that pairs perfectly with the chocolate.

**Hazelnut chocolate muffins.** Divide the batter between 12 lined muffin cups, spooning a little warmed hazelnut spread into each as you fill them, and top each with chopped hazelnuts and a pinch of turbinado sugar. Bake at 350°F for 20-23 minutes. They are perfect for lunchboxes and bake sales.

**Orange hazelnut.** Add the zest of one orange to the wet ingredients. The citrus lifts the dark chocolate and makes the whole loaf taste brighter — a little like a chocolate orange, in banana bread form.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm a slice for 10 seconds in the microwave so the chocolate goes melty again and the crumb turns pillowy. It is wonderful with a smear of salted butter, a spoonful of extra hazelnut spread, or a dollop of mascarpone, and it makes a superb base for a scoop of vanilla or hazelnut ice cream. For the full café experience, pair it with a flat white or a mug of black coffee — the bitterness plays beautifully against the rich sweetness. For dessert, a warm slice with vanilla ice cream and a drizzle of warm hazelnut spread is hard to beat.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days — the sugar top stays crackly for the first day and softens slightly after that, while the crumb stays moist and fudgy. After day 3, refrigerate for up to a week and bring slices back to room temperature before eating. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8-10 minutes or toast them lightly in a dry pan — the edges get deliciously crisp, the chocolate goes melty again, and the whole kitchen smells like a chocolate shop.
