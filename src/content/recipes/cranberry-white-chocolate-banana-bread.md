instructions:
  - "Heat oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x5-inch loaf pan and line with parchment, leaving an overhang on the long sides for easy lifting."
  - "In a large bowl, whisk the mashed bananas, melted butter, granulated sugar, brown sugar, eggs, vanilla, and sour cream until smooth and well combined."
  - "In a separate bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon together."
  - "Toss the halved cranberries and 3/4 cup of the white chocolate chips with the extra tablespoon of flour — this light coating keeps them from sinking to the bottom of the loaf."
  - "Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture until just combined, then gently fold in the flour-coated cranberries and white chocolate chips. A few small lumps in the batter are fine; do not overmix."
  - "Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top. Scatter the remaining 1/4 cup white chocolate chips over the surface and sprinkle with the coarse sugar."
  - "Bake 55–65 minutes, until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs and no raw batter. If the top browns too quickly, tent loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes."
  - "Cool 15 minutes in the pan, then lift out using the parchment overhang and cool completely on a wire rack before slicing."

Tart cranberries and sweet, creamy white chocolate are one of winter's best pairings, and folded into a soft, buttery banana bread they make a loaf that tastes like the holiday season. The cranberries stay bright and jammy against the mellow banana crumb, the white chocolate melts into little creamy pockets, and a sprinkle of coarse sugar on top bakes into a lightly crisp, sparkling crust.

This is a festive loaf without any fuss — one bowl for the wet ingredients, one for the dry, and it comes together in 15 minutes. It looks beautiful on a holiday table, slices cleanly for a cookie platter or a bake sale, and it uses up those spotty bananas on your counter.

### 🌟 Why You'll Love This Recipe

Every slice has ruby-red cranberries and creamy white chocolate in perfect balance — tart against sweet, never one-note. The sour cream keeps the crumb tender and moist for days, the brown sugar adds a gentle caramel warmth, and the coarse sugar top gives a delicate crunch. It bakes up tall and golden, holds its shape when sliced, and the whole house smells like a bakery while it's in the oven.

### Key Ingredients & Baker's Notes

**Very ripe bananas.** Heavily speckled or black-skinned bananas give the sweetest, most flavourful crumb. Mash them until mostly smooth — a few small lumps are fine and add texture.

**Fresh or frozen cranberries.** Halve them so they distribute evenly and bake into jammy pockets rather than whole tart bombs. Use frozen cranberries straight from the freezer; thawing them first makes them bleed colour into the batter.

**White chocolate chips.** Use real white chocolate made with cocoa butter, not candy melts. Three-quarters go into the batter and the rest scatter over the top, where they melt into golden-edged creamy spots.

**Sour cream.** The secret to a tender, bakery-style crumb. Its acidity relaxes the gluten and adds moisture that keeps the loaf soft for days. Full-fat plain Greek yogurt works as a substitute.

**Cinnamon.** Just half a teaspoon — enough to warm the background without competing with the cranberries. Fresh ground cinnamon makes a real difference here.

**Coarse sugar.** A tablespoon or two sprinkled over the top before baking gives a sparkling, lightly crunchy crust. Turbinado or demerara sugar both work.

### Step-by-Step Technique & Method

The only real technique in this loaf is keeping the cranberries suspended, and the flour toss does the work for you. Tossing the halved cranberries and chips in a tablespoon of flour coats them lightly, and that coating grips the batter as it bakes — instead of sinking, the berries stay evenly distributed through every slice.

Fold the dry ingredients in gently and stop as soon as no dry streaks remain. Overmixing develops gluten and bakes up a tough, dense loaf instead of a tender one. A few small lumps in the batter are completely fine.

Start checking at 55 minutes. The toothpick test can be misleading here because a melted white chocolate chip can look wet — look for moist crumbs clinging to the toothpick rather than raw batter. If the top is browning faster than the centre is baking, tent loosely with foil and give it the full time.

### 💡 Expert Tips

- **Halve the cranberries.** Whole cranberries are too big and too tart in a single bite; halved, they distribute evenly and soften into jammy pockets.
- **Line the pan with parchment** with an overhang on the long sides. White chocolate can stick, and the sling lets you lift the whole loaf out cleanly.
- **Room temperature eggs** blend into the batter smoothly instead of seizing the melted butter. Quick fix: put the eggs in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Cool before slicing.** 15 minutes in the pan, then fully on a rack. Slicing too early compresses the soft crumb and the white chocolate smears.
- **For clean slices,** use a serrated bread knife and wipe it between cuts.

### Delicious Variations & Mix-Ins

**Orange zest.** Add the zest of one orange to the wet ingredients for a classic cranberry-orange pairing — bright, citrusy, and very festive.

**Pecan crunch.** Fold in 1/2 cup of chopped toasted pecans with the cranberries for a nutty crunch against the creamy white chocolate.

**Cranberry glaze.** Whisk 1/2 cup powdered sugar with 1–2 tablespoons of cranberry juice until pourable, and drizzle over the cooled loaf for a pretty pink finish.

**Dark chocolate swap.** Not a white chocolate fan? Dark chocolate chunks work too — the loaf becomes richer and less sweet, with the cranberries shining even more.

### How to Serve & Pair

Serve thick slices at room temperature, or warm them for 10 seconds in the microwave to re-melt the white chocolate pockets. It's wonderful with salted butter, a smear of cream cheese, or a drizzle of warm white chocolate sauce for dessert. For breakfast, pair with hot coffee or spiced chai — the tart cranberries cut through the richness beautifully. It also makes a lovely addition to a holiday brunch spread or a festive afternoon tea.

### 📦 Storage, Freezing & Reheating

Store wrapped tightly or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. The sour cream keeps it moist, but after day 3 move it to the fridge for up to a week. To freeze, wrap the whole cooled loaf (or individual slices) in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature. Refresh slices in a 300°F oven for 8–10 minutes or toast them lightly — the edges get deliciously crisp and the white chocolate goes soft again.
